# Mixtures & Alligation — Practice Sets

> Companion to `Mixtures-and-Alligation.md`. Hints point to the section of the notes that contains the intended method — read the hint only after you've been stuck for a minute. Answer key with short solutions is at the bottom.
>
> **TITA** = type-in-the-answer (no options). **MCQ** = choose one.
>
> Suggested timing: 1.5 min/question for Sets A–F, and attempt the Mixed Set in one 15-minute block.
>
> **Run the §2 sanity check on every ratio you produce**: the mean sits closer to whichever ingredient dominates. If your ratio says otherwise, you read the cross straight instead of inverted.

---

## Set A — The alligation cross

**A1.** (MCQ) In what ratio must rice at ₹62/kg be mixed with rice at ₹72/kg so that the mixture is worth ₹64.50/kg?

- (a) 3 : 1
- (b) 1 : 3
- (c) 2 : 1
- (d) 5 : 3

*Hint: §2 — the difference computed on the dearer side belongs to the cheaper quantity.*

**A2.** (TITA) Two varieties of sugar costing ₹28/kg and ₹40/kg are mixed in the ratio 5 : 3. Find the cost (in ₹) per kg of the mixture.

*Hint: §2 run forwards — this is a plain weighted average, no cross needed.*

**A3.** (TITA) In what ratio must water be mixed with milk costing ₹32/litre so that the seller gains 25% by selling the mixture at ₹32/litre? The ratio water : milk is 1 : k — find k.

*Hint: §4 — first convert the 25% gain into the effective CP of the mixture, then set c = 0 for water.*

**A4.** (MCQ) In what ratio should pulses worth ₹15/kg and ₹20/kg be mixed to get a mixture worth ₹16.50/kg?

- (a) 7 : 3
- (b) 3 : 7
- (c) 5 : 2
- (d) 2 : 5

*Hint: §2 — the mean is much closer to 15, so the ₹15 variety must dominate heavily.*

**A5.** (TITA) 8 kg of a variety costing ₹40/kg is mixed with 12 kg of a variety costing ₹65/kg. Find the price (in ₹) per kg of the mixture.

*Hint: §2 forwards — total cost over total weight.*

## Set B — Concentration, water and adulteration

**B1.** (TITA) How much water (in litres) must be added to 60 litres of a 20% acid solution to bring it down to 15%?

*Hint: §4, third worked example — water is 0%. The cross gives a ratio; the 60 litres anchors it.*

**B2.** (MCQ) A 30% alcohol solution is mixed with a 60% alcohol solution to obtain a 50% solution. The ratio is

- (a) 1 : 2
- (b) 2 : 1
- (c) 1 : 3
- (d) 3 : 1

*Hint: §4 — treat concentration exactly as you would a price.*

**B3.** (TITA) A 50-litre solution contains 8% salt. How much water (in litres) must evaporate so that the solution becomes 10% salt?

*Hint: §7, last worked example — the salt is unchanged. Anchor on the fixed component instead of using the cross.*

**B4.** (MCQ) A vessel holds 80 litres of a milk–water mixture containing 12.5% water. How much milk must be added to reduce the water to 10% of the mixture?

- (a) 15 litres
- (b) 20 litres
- (c) 25 litres
- (d) 30 litres

*Hint: §7 — the water quantity never changes, so work out what total makes it 10%.*

**B5.** (TITA) A milkman mixes water with milk costing ₹45/litre and sells the mixture at ₹45/litre, gaining 12.5%. How many litres of water does he use per 100 litres of milk?

*Hint: §4 — SP = CP of milk means the whole gain comes from the water. Get the water : milk ratio, then scale to 100.*

## Set C — Repeated replacement

**C1.** (TITA) A vessel contains 50 litres of pure milk. 10 litres are removed and replaced with water; this is done twice in all. Find the milk (in litres) remaining.

*Hint: §5 — final pure liquid = L(1 − x/L)ⁿ.*

**C2.** (MCQ) From a cask of 64 litres of wine, 8 litres are drawn out and replaced by water. This is done three times in all. The ratio of wine to water in the cask is now

- (a) 343 : 169
- (b) 512 : 343
- (c) 343 : 512
- (d) 7 : 1

*Hint: §5 — compute the wine first, then subtract from 64 to get the water.*

**C3.** (TITA) From 729 litres of pure milk, 243 litres are drawn off and replaced with water each time. After how many such operations is the milk down to 216 litres?

*Hint: §5, second worked example — the ratio will be a clean power. Find the factor per operation first.*

**C4.** (TITA) A container has 40 litres of milk. 4 litres are withdrawn and replaced by water, twice. Find the amount of water (in litres) in the final mixture.

*Hint: §5 — the formula gives the milk. The question asks for the water.*

**C5.** (MCQ) A 20-litre vessel is full of pure spirit. 5 litres are removed and replaced with water; then 5 litres of the resulting mixture are removed and replaced with water. What fraction of the final mixture is spirit?

- (a) 9/16
- (b) 3/4
- (c) 1/2
- (d) 7/16

*Hint: §5 — as a ratio the formula reads ((L − x)/L)ⁿ, and no absolute volume is needed.*

**C6.** (TITA) A 90-litre mixture is 80% milk. 18 litres of the mixture are removed and replaced with water. Find the percentage of milk in the new mixture.

*Hint: §5, the variant at the end — apply the factor to the milk you actually have, not to the vessel's capacity.*

## Set D — Mixing two mixtures

**D1.** (MCQ) Vessel A has milk : water = 5 : 1 and vessel B has milk : water = 3 : 2. In what ratio must A and B be mixed to get milk : water = 2 : 1?

- (a) 2 : 5
- (b) 5 : 2
- (c) 1 : 2
- (d) 3 : 2

*Hint: §7 — convert all three to milk fractions before touching the cross. Never put raw ratio numbers into it.*

**D2.** (TITA) A 60-litre mixture has milk and water in the ratio 7 : 3. How much water (in litres) must be added to make the ratio 3 : 2?

*Hint: §7, last worked example — the milk is fixed, so anchor on it.*

**D3.** (MCQ) Two vessels contain spirit and water: the first in the ratio 8 : 3, the second in the ratio 5 : 1. In what ratio must they be mixed to obtain spirit : water = 4 : 1?

- (a) 11 : 24
- (b) 24 : 11
- (c) 3 : 2
- (d) 2 : 3

*Hint: §7 — spirit fractions are 8/11, 5/6 and 4/5. The fractions are close together, so keep exact values, don't round.*

**D4.** (TITA) 20 litres of a mixture with milk : water = 3 : 1 is mixed with 30 litres of a mixture with milk : water = 2 : 3. Find the percentage of milk in the result.

*Hint: §7 — total milk over total volume is faster here than the cross, since both quantities are given.*

**D5.** (TITA) A 40-litre mixture has milk : water = 3 : 2. How many litres of the mixture must be removed and replaced with pure milk so that milk : water becomes 4 : 1?

*Hint: §5 logic in reverse — the volume stays 40, so track the water, which only ever decreases.*

## Set E — Alligation on profit, interest, ages and speed

**E1.** (TITA) A man invests ₹12,000, part at 10% and the rest at 14%, earning 11.5% overall. Find the amount (in ₹) invested at 10%.

*Hint: §8, first worked example — interest rates are weighted by principal.*

**E2.** (MCQ) A shopkeeper sells part of 100 kg of sugar at 12% profit and the rest at 20% profit, making 15% overall. The quantity sold at 20% profit is

- (a) 30 kg
- (b) 37.5 kg
- (c) 40 kg
- (d) 62.5 kg

*Hint: §8 — profit % is weighted by cost price. Note which branch of the cross belongs to which quantity.*

**E3.** (TITA) The average age of 40 students is 15 years. The boys average 16 and the girls average 12. Find the number of girls.

*Hint: §8, second worked example. Run the integer check on your answer.*

**E4.** (MCQ) A trader buys 30 litres of oil at ₹80/litre and some quantity at ₹95/litre. He sells the whole lot at ₹99/litre, making 10% profit. The quantity bought at ₹95/litre is

- (a) 45 litres
- (b) 50 litres
- (c) 60 litres
- (d) 75 litres

*Hint: §8 — back out the CP of the mixture from the 10% profit, then alligate. The 30 litres anchors the ratio.*

**E5.** (TITA) A car covers a 5-hour journey partly at 40 km/h and partly at 60 km/h, averaging 46 km/h. For how many hours did it travel at 60 km/h?

*Hint: §3 — alligation on speed is legal only when weighted by time, and here the total given is a time.*

## Set F — Harder blends and three ingredients

**F1.** (TITA) Three varieties of tea costing ₹126, ₹135 and ₹175 per kg are mixed in the ratio 1 : 1 : 2. Find the cost (in ₹) per kg of the mixture.

*Hint: §6 — with the ratio already given, the weighted-average equation beats forcing the cross.*

**F2.** (MCQ) In what ratio must pulses at ₹15/kg and ₹20/kg be mixed so that selling the mixture at ₹21.60/kg gives a 20% gain?

- (a) 2 : 3
- (b) 3 : 2
- (c) 1 : 2
- (d) 4 : 5

*Hint: §8 — the SP is a distractor until you convert it to the mixture's CP.*

**F3.** (TITA) A mixture of 80 litres contains milk and water in the ratio 3 : 1. How much milk (in litres) must be added so the ratio becomes 4 : 1?

*Hint: §7 — the water is the fixed component this time.*

**F4.** (MCQ) A dishonest milkman sells milk at cost price but mixes in water and gains 25%. Water as a percentage of the mixture is

- (a) 20%
- (b) 25%
- (c) 16.67%
- (d) 33.33%

*Hint: §4 and the last row of the Traps table — "water : milk" and "water as a share of the mixture" are different numbers.*

**F5.** (TITA) A vessel contains liquids A and B in the ratio 7 : 5. When 9 litres of the mixture are drawn off and replaced with B, the ratio becomes 7 : 9. Find the initial quantity (in litres) of A.

*Hint: §5 conditions — the replacement liquid is B, not water, so the standard formula does not apply. Let the total be 12k and track each liquid.*

**F6.** (MCQ) Can 1 holds a mixture that is 25% water, can 2 holds one that is 50% water. How much should be taken from each to fill a 12-litre can with a mixture that is 40% water?

- (a) 4.8 L and 7.2 L
- (b) 6 L and 6 L
- (c) 7.2 L and 4.8 L
- (d) 5 L and 7 L

*Hint: §4 — alligate on the water percentage, then split the 12 litres in that ratio. Check which can gets which branch.*

## Mixed Set — exam feel (15 minutes)

**M1.** (MCQ) In what ratio must wheat at ₹24/kg be mixed with wheat at ₹36/kg to get a mixture worth ₹30.50/kg?

- (a) 11 : 13
- (b) 13 : 11
- (c) 1 : 2
- (d) 2 : 1

**M2.** (TITA) A 27-litre vessel is full of pure milk. 9 litres are drawn off and replaced with water; this is done three times in all. Find the milk (in litres) remaining.

**M3.** (MCQ) Two alloys contain gold and copper in the ratios 5 : 3 and 5 : 11. In what ratio must they be melted together to get an alloy with gold : copper = 5 : 7?

- (a) 1 : 2
- (b) 2 : 1
- (c) 3 : 5
- (d) 5 : 3

**M4.** (TITA) How much water (in litres) must be added to 24 litres of milk costing ₹32/litre so that the mixture costs ₹24/litre?

**M5.** (MCQ) ₹1,600 is lent partly at 5% and partly at 8% simple interest, and the total annual interest is ₹104. The amount lent at 5% is

- (a) ₹600
- (b) ₹800
- (c) ₹1,000
- (d) ₹1,200

**M6.** (TITA) A 50-litre mixture of milk and water contains 20% water. How much milk (in litres) must be added so that water becomes 10% of the new mixture?

**M7.** (MCQ) A jar contains liquids A and B in the ratio 4 : 1. When 10 litres of the mixture is replaced with liquid B, the ratio becomes 2 : 3. The initial quantity of A was

- (a) 12 litres
- (b) 16 litres
- (c) 20 litres
- (d) 24 litres

**M8.** (TITA) A class of 48 averages 60 marks. The boys average 55 and the girls average 71. Find the number of girls.

**M9.** (MCQ) A container full of pure milk loses 20% of its contents, which is replaced by water, in each of two successive operations. The percentage of milk in the final mixture is

- (a) 60%
- (b) 64%
- (c) 68%
- (d) 80%

**M10.** (TITA) Sugar at ₹7.20/kg is mixed with sugar at ₹5.70/kg so that the mixture, sold at ₹7.20/kg, yields a 20% profit. The ratio of the ₹7.20 variety to the ₹5.70 variety is 1 : k. Find k.

# Answer Key

## Set A

**A1 — (a) 3 : 1.** c = 62, d = 72, m = 64.5. d − m = 7.5, m − c = 2.5 ⟹ cheap : dear = 7.5 : 2.5 = 3 : 1. Check: 64.5 is close to 62, so there must be far more of the cheap variety ✓.

**A2 — ₹32.50.** (5 × 28 + 3 × 40)/8 = (140 + 120)/8 = 260/8 = 32.5.

**A3 — k = 4.** SP = 32 at 25% gain ⟹ CP of mixture = 32/1.25 = 25.6. c = 0, d = 32, m = 25.6. Water : Milk = (32 − 25.6) : 25.6 = 6.4 : 25.6 = 1 : 4.

**A4 — (a) 7 : 3.** (20 − 16.5) : (16.5 − 15) = 3.5 : 1.5 = 7 : 3.

**A5 — ₹55.** (8 × 40 + 12 × 65)/20 = (320 + 780)/20 = 1100/20 = 55.

## Set B

**B1 — 20 litres.** c = 0, d = 20, m = 15 ⟹ water : solution = 5 : 15 = 1 : 3. The solution is 60 litres = 3 parts ⟹ 1 part = 20 litres of water.

**B2 — (a) 1 : 2.** (60 − 50) : (50 − 30) = 10 : 20 = 1 : 2 (the 30% solution first).

**B3 — 10 litres.** Salt = 4 litres and stays fixed. For salt to be 10%, total = 4/0.10 = 40 litres ⟹ evaporate 50 − 40 = 10.

**B4 — (b) 20 litres.** Water = 10 litres and stays fixed. For water to be 10%, total = 100 ⟹ add 100 − 80 = 20 litres of milk.

**B5 — 12.5 litres.** CP of mixture = 45/1.125 = 40. Water : Milk = (45 − 40) : 40 = 5 : 40 = 1 : 8 ⟹ 100 litres of milk needs 12.5 litres of water.

## Set C

**C1 — 32 litres.** 50 × (1 − 10/50)² = 50 × (4/5)² = 50 × 16/25 = 32.

**C2 — (a) 343 : 169.** Wine = 64 × (7/8)³ = 64 × 343/512 = 42.875. Water = 21.125. Ratio = 343 : 169. (Option (c) is the same numbers inverted — a common slip.)

**C3 — 3.** Factor per operation = (729 − 243)/729 = 2/3. 729 × (2/3)ⁿ = 216 ⟹ (2/3)ⁿ = 8/27 ⟹ n = 3.

**C4 — 7.6 litres.** Milk = 40 × (9/10)² = 32.4 ⟹ water = 40 − 32.4 = 7.6.

**C5 — (a) 9/16.** ((20 − 5)/20)² = (3/4)² = 9/16.

**C6 — 64%.** Milk initially = 72 litres. Removing 18 of 90 leaves the factor (90 − 18)/90 = 4/5, so milk = 72 × 4/5 = 57.6 ⟹ 57.6/90 = 64%. (Applying the factor to 90 instead of 72 is the trap.)

## Set D

**D1 — (a) 2 : 5.** Milk fractions: A = 5/6, B = 3/5, target = 2/3. d − m = 5/6 − 2/3 = 1/6; m − c = 2/3 − 3/5 = 1/15. B : A = (1/6) : (1/15) = 15 : 6 = 5 : 2 ⟹ A : B = **2 : 5**. Check: 2 parts A + 5 parts B gives milk 5/3 + 3 = 14/3 out of 7 = 2/3 ✓.

**D2 — 10 litres.** Milk = 42, water = 18. Milk is fixed; for a 3 : 2 ratio milk is 3/5 of the total ⟹ total = 70 ⟹ add 10 litres of water.

**D3 — (a) 11 : 24.** Spirit fractions: first = 8/11, second = 5/6, target = 4/5. d − m = 5/6 − 4/5 = 1/30; m − c = 4/5 − 8/11 = 4/55. first : second = (1/30) : (4/55) = 11 : 24. Check: 11 parts give spirit 8, water 3; 24 parts give spirit 20, water 4 ⟹ 28 : 7 = 4 : 1 ✓.

**D4 — 54%.** Milk = 20 × 3/4 + 30 × 2/5 = 15 + 12 = 27 out of 50 ⟹ 54%.

**D5 — 20 litres.** Water = 16 initially; total stays 40. For milk : water = 4 : 1, water must be 8 litres. Removing x litres of mixture removes 0.4x of water ⟹ 16 − 0.4x = 8 ⟹ x = 20.

## Set E

**E1 — ₹7,500.** (14 − 11.5) : (11.5 − 10) = 2.5 : 1.5 = 5 : 3 ⟹ 10% share = 12000 × 5/8 = 7500.

**E2 — (b) 37.5 kg.** c = 12, d = 20, m = 15. qty at 12% : qty at 20% = (20 − 15) : (15 − 12) = 5 : 3 ⟹ 20% quantity = 100 × 3/8 = 37.5 kg.

**E3 — 10.** Girls : Boys = (16 − 15) : (15 − 12) = 1 : 3. Total 40 = 4 parts ⟹ girls = 10, boys = 30. Check: (10 × 12 + 30 × 16)/40 = 600/40 = 15 ✓.

**E4 — (c) 60 litres.** CP of mixture = 99/1.1 = 90. (95 − 90) : (90 − 80) = 5 : 10 = 1 : 2, i.e. ₹80 : ₹95 = 1 : 2 ⟹ 30 litres at ₹80 means 60 litres at ₹95.

**E5 — 1.5 hours.** t₄₀ : t₆₀ = (60 − 46) : (46 − 40) = 14 : 6 = 7 : 3. Total 5 h = 10 parts ⟹ t₆₀ = 1.5 h. (Legal because the weights here are times, not distances.)

## Set F

**F1 — ₹152.75.** (1 × 126 + 1 × 135 + 2 × 175)/4 = (126 + 135 + 350)/4 = 611/4 = 152.75.

**F2 — (a) 2 : 3.** CP of mixture = 21.60/1.2 = 18. (20 − 18) : (18 − 15) = 2 : 3.

**F3 — 20 litres.** Milk = 60, water = 20 and fixed. For 4 : 1, water is 1/5 of the total ⟹ total = 100 ⟹ add 20 litres of milk.

**F4 — (a) 20%.** Selling at CP with a 25% gain means water : milk = 1 : 4, so water is 1/5 = 20% of the mixture. (25% is the trap — that's water as a percentage of the *milk*.)

**F5 — 21 litres.** Let total = 12k, so A = 7k, B = 5k. Drawing 9 litres removes 9 × 7/12 = 21/4 of A and 9 × 5/12 = 15/4 of B, and 9 litres of B go back in. (7k − 21/4)/(5k + 21/4) = 7/9 ⟹ 63k − 189/4 = 35k + 147/4 ⟹ 28k = 84 ⟹ k = 3 ⟹ A = 21.

**F6 — (a) 4.8 L and 7.2 L.** Alligate on water: c = 25, d = 50, m = 40 ⟹ can 1 : can 2 = (50 − 40) : (40 − 25) = 10 : 15 = 2 : 3. Of 12 litres: 4.8 from can 1 and 7.2 from can 2. Check: water = 1.2 + 3.6 = 4.8 = 40% of 12 ✓.

## Mixed Set

**M1 — (a) 11 : 13.** (36 − 30.5) : (30.5 − 24) = 5.5 : 6.5 = 11 : 13.

**M2 — 8 litres.** 27 × (1 − 9/27)³ = 27 × (2/3)³ = 27 × 8/27 = 8.

**M3 — (a) 1 : 2.** Gold fractions: 5/8, 5/16, target 5/12. d − m = 5/8 − 5/12 = 5/24; m − c = 5/12 − 5/16 = 5/48. second : first = (5/24) : (5/48) = 2 : 1 ⟹ first : second = 1 : 2.

**M4 — 8 litres.** c = 0, d = 32, m = 24 ⟹ water : milk = 8 : 24 = 1 : 3. Milk = 24 ⟹ water = 8.

**M5 — (b) ₹800.** Overall rate = 104/1600 = 6.5%. (8 − 6.5) : (6.5 − 5) = 1 : 1 ⟹ ₹800 at each rate.

**M6 — 50 litres.** Water = 10 litres and fixed. For water to be 10%, total = 100 ⟹ add 50 litres of milk.

**M7 — (b) 16 litres.** Total = 5k, A = 4k, B = k. Removing 10 litres takes out 8 of A and 2 of B, and 10 of B go back. (4k − 8)/(k + 8) = 2/3 ⟹ 12k − 24 = 2k + 16 ⟹ k = 4 ⟹ A = 16.

**M8 — 15.** Boys : Girls = (71 − 60) : (60 − 55) = 11 : 5. Total 48 = 16 parts ⟹ girls = 15, boys = 33. Check: (33 × 55 + 15 × 71)/48 = 2880/48 = 60 ✓.

**M9 — (b) 64%.** Each operation multiplies the milk by 0.8 ⟹ (0.8)² = 0.64.

**M10 — k = 4.** CP of mixture = 7.20/1.2 = 6. c = 5.70, d = 7.20, m = 6. cheap : dear = (7.2 − 6) : (6 − 5.7) = 1.2 : 0.3 = 4 : 1, so ₹7.20 : ₹5.70 = 1 : 4.

**Common error audit** — if you got a question wrong, find it here before moving on:

| Question | The error it is designed to catch |
|---|---|
| A1, A4, M1 | reading the cross straight instead of inverted |
| A3, B5, F2, F4, M10 | using the SP where the mixture's CP is needed |
| C2, C4 | computing the pure liquid and forgetting the question asked for water |
| C6 | applying the replacement factor to the vessel's capacity instead of the impure contents |
| D1, D3, M3 | putting raw ratio numbers into the cross instead of fractions |
| D2, B3, B4, F3, M6 | using the cross when anchoring on the fixed component is faster and safer |
| D5, F5, M7 | using the replacement formula where it does not apply |
| E5 | alligating speed over distance rather than time |
| E3, M8 | ignoring the integer check on a head count |
| F4 | confusing "% of the mixture" with "% of the milk" |
