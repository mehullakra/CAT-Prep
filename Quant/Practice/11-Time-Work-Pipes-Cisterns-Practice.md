# Time, Work, Pipes & Cisterns — Practice Sets

> Companion to `11-Time-Work-Pipes-Cisterns.md`. Hints point to the section of the notes that contains the intended method — read the hint only after you've been stuck for a minute. Answer key with short solutions is at the bottom.
>
> **TITA** = type-in-the-answer (no options). **MCQ** = choose one.
>
> Suggested timing: 1.5 min/question for Sets A–F, and attempt the Mixed Set in one timed block.
>
> **Assume the total work is the LCM of the given times.** Every rate then becomes a whole number and the fractions disappear from the entire solution (§1).
>
> A pipe that empties is a negative rate. Nothing else about §7 differs from ordinary work problems.

---

## Set A — The LCM method and efficiency

**A1.** (TITA) A can do a job in 12 days and B in 18 days. Working together, how many days do they take?

*Hint: §1 — take the total work as LCM(12, 18) = 36 units.*

**A2.** (MCQ) A and B together do a job in 8 days; A alone takes 12 days. B alone takes

- (a) 16 days
- (b) 20 days
- (c) 24 days
- (d) 30 days

*Hint: §1 — LCM(8, 12) = 24. Subtract rates, never times.*

**A3.** (TITA) A is twice as efficient as B, and together they finish a job in 12 days. How many days does A alone take?

*Hint: §2 — efficiency ratio 2 : 1 means the combined rate is 3 parts, of which A supplies 2.*

**A4.** (MCQ) A, B and C can do a job in 10, 12 and 15 days respectively. Working together they take

- (a) 4 days
- (b) 5 days
- (c) 6 days
- (d) 8 days

*Hint: §1 — LCM(10, 12, 15) = 60.*

**A5.** (TITA) A can do a job in 20 days and B in 30 days. They work together for 6 days, then A leaves. How many more days does B need?

*Hint: §11 — count the units done, then divide the remainder by B's rate alone.*

**A6.** (MCQ) If 3 men can do a piece of work in 8 days, then 4 men take

- (a) 4 days
- (b) 6 days
- (c) 8 days
- (d) 10.67 days

*Hint: §3 — total man-days is fixed. More men means fewer days: an inverse proportion.*

## Set B — Men, days and hours (the M-D-H formula)

**B1.** (TITA) If 12 men working 8 hours a day complete a job in 15 days, how many days will 20 men working 9 hours a day take?

*Hint: §3 — men × days × hours is a constant for a fixed job.*

**B2.** (MCQ) 15 workers build a wall in 48 days. The number of workers needed to build it in 30 days is

- (a) 20
- (b) 22
- (c) 24
- (d) 28

*Hint: §3 — men and days are inversely proportional.*

**B3.** (TITA) 6 men and 8 boys can do a job in 10 days, while 26 men and 48 boys can do it in 2 days. How many days would 15 men and 20 boys take?

*Hint: §10 — let m and b be the daily rates of one man and one boy, form two equations and eliminate.*

**B4.** (MCQ) If 8 machines running 6 hours a day produce 480 units in 5 days, then 12 machines running 8 hours a day produce, in 3 days,

- (a) 432 units
- (b) 480 units
- (c) 576 units
- (d) 640 units

*Hint: §3 — find the output per machine-hour, then scale up. Output is directly proportional here, not inversely.*

**B5.** (TITA) 10 men can complete a work in 12 days. After 4 days, 5 more men join. In how many more days is the work finished? Give the answer to two decimals.

*Hint: §5 — convert to man-days, subtract what's done, divide by the new workforce.*

## Set C — Alternate days and rotation cycles

**C1.** (TITA) A can do a job in 10 days and B in 15 days. They work on alternate days starting with A. How many days does the job take?

*Hint: §4 — find the work done per 2-day cycle, run whole cycles, then finish day by day.*

**C2.** (MCQ) A can do a job in 12 days and B in 24 days. Working on alternate days starting with B, the job takes

- (a) 15 days
- (b) 16 days
- (c) 17 days
- (d) 18 days

*Hint: §4 — who starts changes the cycle order but not its total. Check whether the job ends exactly on a cycle boundary.*

**C3.** (TITA) A, B and C can do a job in 6, 12 and 24 days. They work in rotation, one day each, starting with A. How many days does the job take?

*Hint: §4 — three-day cycle. When the last cycle overshoots, take the fractional part of that worker's day.*

**C4.** (MCQ) A does a job in 9 days and B in 18 days. Working alternately starting with A, the work is completed in

- (a) 11 days
- (b) 12 days
- (c) 13 days
- (d) 12.5 days

*Hint: §4 — check whether the cycles land exactly on the total.*

**C5.** (TITA) Two taps fill a tank in 20 and 30 minutes. They are opened alternately for one minute each, starting with the first. How many minutes does the tank take to fill?

*Hint: §4 + §7 — identical machinery, minutes instead of days.*

## Set D — Joining, leaving and partial work

**D1.** (TITA) A can do a job in 15 days and B in 20 days. They start together but A leaves after 4 days. In how many more days does B finish? Give the answer to two decimals.

*Hint: §5 — units done, then remainder over B's rate.*

**D2.** (MCQ) A and B together do a job in 12 days. They work for 8 days, then A leaves and B finishes the rest in 6 days. B alone would take

- (a) 15 days
- (b) 18 days
- (c) 20 days
- (d) 24 days

*Hint: §11 — the fraction B does alone, divided by the time it took, gives B's rate.*

**D3.** (TITA) A can do a job in 24 days. He works for 6 days, then B finishes the remaining work in 9 days. How many days would B alone take for the whole job?

*Hint: §11 — find the fraction left, then scale B's 9 days up to the whole job.*

**D4.** (MCQ) 20 men start a job scheduled to take 30 days. After 10 days, 5 men leave. The total time taken is

- (a) 36 days
- (b) 36.67 days
- (c) 40 days
- (d) 45 days

*Hint: §5 — work in man-days throughout, and remember to add back the 10 days already spent.*

**D5.** (TITA) A, B and C together do a job in 8 days. A alone takes 24 days and B alone 32 days. How many days does C alone take?

*Hint: §1 — take LCM(8, 24, 32) = 96 and subtract rates.*

## Set E — Pipes and cisterns

**E1.** (TITA) A pipe fills a tank in 6 hours and another empties it in 9 hours. With both open, how many hours does the tank take to fill?

*Hint: §7 — the emptying pipe is a negative rate. Take LCM(6, 9) = 18.*

**E2.** (MCQ) Two pipes fill a tank in 12 and 15 minutes; a waste pipe empties it in 20 minutes. With all three open, the tank fills in

- (a) 8 min
- (b) 10 min
- (c) 12 min
- (d) 15 min

*Hint: §7 — LCM(12, 15, 20) = 60; add the fillers and subtract the drain.*

**E3.** (TITA) A tank is filled by pipe A in 20 minutes and by pipe B in 30 minutes. Both are opened, but A is closed after 5 minutes. How many more minutes does B need?

*Hint: §7 + §5 — the same leave-midway structure as D1.*

**E4.** (MCQ) A leak empties a full cistern in 8 hours. With a tap admitting 6 litres a minute turned on, the cistern empties in 12 hours instead. Its capacity is

- (a) 7,200 litres
- (b) 8,640 litres
- (c) 9,600 litres
- (d) 10,800 litres

*Hint: §7 — the tap's rate is the difference of the two emptying rates. Convert litres per minute to litres per hour at the end.*

**E5.** (TITA) Two pipes fill a tank in 15 and 20 hours. Both are opened together, but the first is closed after 4 hours. In how many more hours is the tank full? Give the answer to two decimals.

*Hint: §7 — LCM(15, 20) = 60.*

---

## Set F — Pairwise rates, ratios and wages

**F1.** (TITA) A and B can do a job in 12 days, B and C in 15 days, and C and A in 20 days. How many days would all three together take?

*Hint: §9 — add all three pairwise rates; the sum counts each worker twice.*

**F2.** (MCQ) A and B can do a job in 12 days, B and C in 15 days, and C and A in 20 days. A alone would take

- (a) 24 days
- (b) 30 days
- (c) 36 days
- (d) 45 days

*Hint: §9 — get the three-person rate first, then subtract the pair that excludes A.*

**F3.** (TITA) A and B take on a job for ₹3,200. A alone can do it in 12 days and B in 16 days. With C's help they finish in 6 days. Find C's share (in ₹).

*Hint: §6 — wages split in the ratio of work done, which is the ratio of rates when all work the same number of days.*

**F4.** (MCQ) A does 3/5 of a job in 9 days. He then calls B, and together they finish the rest in 4 days. B alone would take

- (a) 20 days
- (b) 24 days
- (c) 30 days
- (d) 36 days

*Hint: §8 — convert each statement into a rate, then subtract A's rate from the combined rate.*

**F5.** (TITA) A is 60% more efficient than B. If B alone takes 40 days, how many days do they take together? Give the answer to two decimals.

*Hint: §2 — "60% more efficient" scales the rate, not the time. A's rate is 1.6 times B's.*

## Mixed Set — exam feel (15 minutes)

**M1.** (TITA) A can do a job in 16 days and B in 24 days. Working together, how many days do they take?

**M2.** (MCQ) If 18 men can build a wall in 20 days, then 24 men can build it in

- (a) 12 days
- (b) 15 days
- (c) 16 days
- (d) 18 days

**M3.** (TITA) A pipe fills a tank in 10 hours while a leak empties the full tank in 15 hours. With both operating, how many hours does the tank take to fill?

**M4.** (MCQ) A and B can complete a job in 10 days, B and C in 12 days, and A and C in 15 days. B alone would take

- (a) 17.14 days
- (b) 20 days
- (c) 24 days
- (d) 30 days

**M5.** (TITA) A does a job in 18 days and B in 12 days. Working alternately starting with B, how many days does the job take? Give the answer to two decimals.

**M6.** (MCQ) 4 men and 6 women complete a job in 8 days, while 3 men and 7 women complete it in 10 days. The number of days 10 women take is

- (a) 30
- (b) 35
- (c) 40
- (d) 50

**M7.** (TITA) A cistern is filled by two pipes in 12 and 16 minutes. Both are opened together; after how many minutes should the first be closed so that the tank is exactly full in 10 minutes?

**M8.** (MCQ) A, B and C earn ₹5,400 for a job in which A and B together do 7/12 of the work. C's share is

- (a) ₹2,250
- (b) ₹2,700
- (c) ₹3,150
- (d) ₹3,600

**M9.** (TITA) A is thrice as good a workman as B, and together they finish a job in 15 days. How many days does B alone take?

**M10.** (MCQ) 12 men complete 2/5 of a job in 8 days. The number of additional men needed to finish the rest in 6 days is

- (a) 8
- (b) 10
- (c) 12
- (d) 16

# Answer key

## Set A

**A1 — 7.2 days.** Rates 3 and 2 units/day ⟹ 5 together ⟹ 36/5 = **7.2 days**.

**A2 — (c) 24 days.** Total 24 units: together 3/day, A 2/day ⟹ B = 1/day ⟹ **24 days**. (Subtracting 12 from 8 is meaningless — rates subtract, times don't.)

**A3 — 18 days.** Combined = 3 parts in 12 days ⟹ A's 2 parts take 12 × 3/2 = **18 days** (B takes 36).

**A4 — (a) 4 days.** Rates 6, 5 and 4 ⟹ 15/day ⟹ 60/15 = **4 days**.

**A5 — 15 days.** Total 60 units, rates 3 and 2. Six days together = 30 units ⟹ 30 remain ⟹ B needs 30/2 = **15 days**.

**A6 — (b) 6 days.** 3 × 8 = 24 man-days ⟹ 24/4 = **6 days**.

## Set B

**B1 — 8 days.** 12 × 8 × 15 = 1440 man-hours ⟹ 1440/(20 × 9) = **8 days**.

**B2 — (c) 24.** 15 × 48 = 720 worker-days ⟹ 720/30 = **24 workers**.

**B3 — 4 days.** 60m + 80b = 1 and 52m + 96b = 1 ⟹ b = 1/200 and m = 1/100. Then 15m + 20b = 0.15 + 0.10 = 0.25 ⟹ **4 days**.

**B4 — (c) 576 units.** 480/(8 × 6 × 5) = 2 units per machine-hour ⟹ 12 × 8 × 3 × 2 = **576 units**.

**B5 — 5.33 days.** Total 120 man-days; 40 done ⟹ 80 remain with 15 men ⟹ 80/15 = **5.33 days**.

## Set C

**C1 — 12 days.** Total 30 units; A 3/day, B 2/day ⟹ 5 per cycle. Five cycles (10 days) = 25 units. Day 11: A adds 3 ⟹ 28. Day 12: B adds 2 ⟹ 30 ⟹ **12 days**.

**C2 — (b) 16 days.** Total 24 units; A 2, B 1 ⟹ 3 per cycle. Eight cycles = 16 days and exactly 24 units ⟹ **16 days**.

**C3 — 9.75 days.** Total 24 units; rates 4, 2, 1 ⟹ 7 per 3-day cycle. Three cycles (9 days) = 21 units ⟹ 3 remain and A does 4/day ⟹ ¾ of a day ⟹ **9.75 days**.

**C4 — (b) 12 days.** Total 18 units; A 2, B 1 ⟹ 3 per cycle. Six cycles = 12 days and exactly 18 units ⟹ **12 days**.

**C5 — 24 minutes.** Total 60 units; rates 3 and 2 ⟹ 5 per 2-minute cycle. Eleven cycles (22 min) = 55 units. Minute 23: +3 ⟹ 58. Minute 24: +2 ⟹ 60 ⟹ **24 minutes**.

## Set D

**D1 — 10.67 days.** Total 60 units; rates 4 and 3. Four days together = 28 ⟹ 32 remain ⟹ 32/3 = **10.67 days**.

**D2 — (b) 18 days.** In 8 days they finish 8/12 = 2/3 ⟹ B does the remaining 1/3 in 6 days ⟹ B alone takes **18 days**.

**D3 — 12 days.** A does 6/24 = 1/4 ⟹ 3/4 remains, done by B in 9 days ⟹ B alone takes 9 ÷ 3/4 = **12 days**.

**D4 — (b) 36.67 days.** Total 600 man-days; 200 done ⟹ 400 remain with 15 men ⟹ 26.67 more days ⟹ **36.67 days** in total.

**D5 — 19.2 days.** Total 96 units: together 12/day, A 4, B 3 ⟹ C = 5/day ⟹ 96/5 = **19.2 days**.

## Set E

**E1 — 18 hours.** Rates +3 and −2 ⟹ net 1 unit/hour ⟹ **18 hours**.

**E2 — (b) 10 min.** Rates +5, +4, −3 ⟹ net 6/min ⟹ 60/6 = **10 minutes**.

**E3 — 17.5 minutes.** Total 60 units; rates 3 and 2. Five minutes together = 25 ⟹ 35 remain ⟹ 35/2 = **17.5 minutes**.

**E4 — (b) 8,640 litres.** Tap rate = 1/8 − 1/12 = 1/24 of the tank per hour ⟹ the tap alone would fill it in 24 hours at 360 litres/hour ⟹ capacity = **8,640 litres**.

**E5 — 10.67 hours.** Rates 4 and 3. Four hours together = 28 units ⟹ 32 remain ⟹ 32/3 = **10.67 hours**.

## Set F

**F1 — 10 days.** Total 60 units: A+B = 5, B+C = 4, C+A = 3 ⟹ 2(A+B+C) = 12 ⟹ 6/day ⟹ **10 days**.

**F2 — (b) 30 days.** A+B+C = 6 units/day and B+C = 4 ⟹ A = 2/day ⟹ 60/2 = **30 days**.

**F3 — ₹400.** Total 48 units: A 4, B 3, all three 8 ⟹ C = 1/day. Ratio 4 : 3 : 1 ⟹ C gets 3200/8 = **₹400**.

**F4 — (c) 30 days.** A's rate = (3/5)/9 = 1/15. Remaining 2/5 in 4 days ⟹ combined = 1/10 ⟹ B = 1/10 − 1/15 = 1/30 ⟹ **30 days**.

**F5 — 15.38 days.** B = 1/40, A = 1.6/40 = 1/25 ⟹ combined = 13/200 ⟹ 200/13 = **15.38 days**. (A alone takes 25 days, not 40/1.6 rounded carelessly.)

## Mixed Set

**M1 — 9.6 days.** Rates 3 and 2 ⟹ 5/day ⟹ 48/5 = **9.6 days**.

**M2 — (b) 15 days.** 360/24 = **15 days**.

**M3 — 30 hours.** +3 and −2 ⟹ net 1/hour ⟹ **30 hours**.

**M4 — (a) 17.14 days.** Total 60 units: 6 + 5 + 4 = 15 ⟹ A+B+C = 7.5/day. A+C = 4 ⟹ B = 3.5 ⟹ 60/3.5 = **17.14 days**.

**M5 — 14.33 days.** Total 36 units; A 2, B 3 ⟹ 5 per cycle. Seven cycles (14 days) = 35 ⟹ 1 unit remains and B does 3/day ⟹ ⅓ day ⟹ **14.33 days**.

**M6 — (c) 40.** 32m + 48w = 1 and 30m + 70w = 1 ⟹ w = 1/400 ⟹ 10 women work at 1/40 per day ⟹ **40 days**.

**M7 — 4.5 minutes.** t/12 + 10/16 = 1 ⟹ t/12 = 0.375 ⟹ t = **4.5 minutes**.

**M8 — (a) ₹2,250.** C does 5/12 ⟹ 5400 × 5/12 = **₹2,250**.

**M9 — 60 days.** 4 parts take 15 days ⟹ B's 1 part takes **60 days** (A takes 20).

**M10 — (c) 12.** 96 man-days for 2/5 ⟹ 240 for the whole ⟹ 144 remain in 6 days ⟹ 24 men needed ⟹ **12 additional** men. (24 is the total, planted as an option elsewhere in this style.)

**Common error audit** — if you got a question wrong, find it here before moving on:

| Question | The error it is designed to catch |
|---|---|
| A2, D5, F1, F2, M4 | subtracting times instead of rates — only rates add and subtract (§1, §9) |
| A3, F5, M9 | applying an efficiency ratio to the times rather than to the rates (§2) |
| A6, B1, B2, B5, D4, M2 | losing track of man-days, or forgetting to add the days already elapsed (§3, §5) |
| B3, M6 | trying to solve two-worker-type problems with one variable |
| C1, C3, C5, M5 | running whole cycles past the finish instead of stopping at the fractional day (§4) |
| C2, C4 | assuming a fractional day is always needed — sometimes the cycles land exactly |
| D2, D3, F4 | using the elapsed time as a rate instead of converting the fraction of work done (§8, §11) |
| E1, E2, M3 | adding the emptying pipe instead of subtracting it (§7) |
| E4 | forgetting to convert litres per minute into the same time unit as the rest of the problem |
| F3, M8 | splitting wages equally or by days worked instead of by work done (§6) |
| M7 | assuming both pipes run for the same time |
| M10 | reporting the total workforce where the question asked for the additional men |
