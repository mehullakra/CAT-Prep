# Progressions (AP & GP) — Practice Sets

> Companion to `Progressions-AP-GP.md`. Hints point to the section of the notes that contains the intended method — read the hint only after you've been stuck for a minute. Answer key with short solutions is at the bottom.
>
> **TITA** = type-in-the-answer (no options). **MCQ** = choose one.
>
> Suggested timing: 1.5 min/question for Sets A–E, and attempt the Mixed Set in one timed block.
>
> **For an AP, find d first and the first term second.** Two given terms always fix d by subtraction, and everything else follows (§1).
>
> The single most useful AP fact: the sum of n terms equals n × (middle term) when n is odd. It turns many "sum of 15 terms" questions into one multiplication (§4).

---

## Set A — AP basics

**A1.** (TITA) Find the 20th term of the AP 7, 11, 15, …

*Hint: §1 — aₙ = a + (n − 1)d. The trap is using n rather than n − 1.*

**A2.** (MCQ) The number of terms in the AP 5, 9, 13, …, 185 is

- (a) 44
- (b) 45
- (c) 46
- (d) 47

*Hint: §1 — n = (last − first)/d + 1. Forgetting the +1 is the standard slip.*

**A3.** (TITA) If the 5th term of an AP is 17 and the 9th is 29, find the first term.

*Hint: §1 — the gap between the 5th and 9th terms is 4d, not 5d.*

**A4.** (MCQ) In an AP, the sum of the 4th and 12th terms is 40. The sum of the first 15 terms is

- (a) 200
- (b) 250
- (c) 300
- (d) 400

*Hint: §4 — terms equidistant from the ends sum to the same value, and the 8th term is the average of the 4th and 12th.*

**A5.** (TITA) The 7th term of an AP is 34 and the 13th is 64. Find the 20th term.

*Hint: §1 — six gaps between the 7th and 13th terms, then seven more to the 20th.*

## Set B — Sums and term ranges

**B1.** (TITA) Find the sum of the first 30 terms of the AP 3, 7, 11, …

*Hint: §2 — Sₙ = n/2 [2a + (n − 1)d].*

**B2.** (MCQ) The sum of all two-digit numbers divisible by 7 is

- (a) 700
- (b) 728
- (c) 735
- (d) 770

*Hint: §2 — the terms run 14 to 98. Count them first, then use n/2 (first + last).*

**B3.** (TITA) Find the sum of the terms from the 11th to the 20th of the AP 2, 5, 8, …

*Hint: §2 — a term range is S₂₀ − S₁₀. Do not try to build a new AP from scratch.*

**B4.** (MCQ) The sum of the first n natural numbers is 210. Then n is

- (a) 18
- (b) 19
- (c) 20
- (d) 21

*Hint: §4 — n(n + 1)/2 = 210, so n(n + 1) = 420. Look for consecutive integers near √420.*

**B5.** (TITA) The sum of the first 12 terms of an AP is 390 and the first term is 5. Find the common difference.

*Hint: §2 — substitute into S₁₂ = 6[2a + 11d] and solve the single linear equation.*

**B6.** (MCQ) The sum of the first 20 odd natural numbers is

- (a) 380
- (b) 400
- (c) 420
- (d) 441

*Hint: §4 — the sum of the first n odd numbers is exactly n².*

## Set C — Counting and locating terms

**C1.** (TITA) How many terms of the AP 3, 7, 11, … are less than 200?

*Hint: §3 — write the general term, solve the inequality, then take the floor.*

**C2.** (MCQ) How many three-digit numbers are divisible by 6?

- (a) 148
- (b) 149
- (c) 150
- (d) 151

*Hint: §3 — the range is 102 to 996.*

**C3.** (TITA) How many numbers between 100 and 400 are divisible by both 4 and 6?

*Hint: §3 — divisible by both means divisible by the LCM, 12, not 24.*

**C4.** (MCQ) How many terms are common to the APs 2, 5, 8, … and 3, 7, 11, …, taking both only up to 200?

- (a) 14
- (b) 15
- (c) 16
- (d) 17

*Hint: §3 — common terms form their own AP whose difference is the LCM of the two differences. Find the first common term by inspection.*

**C5.** (TITA) How many integers strictly between 1 and 500 are divisible by 3 but not by 5?

*Hint: §3 — count the multiples of 3, then subtract the multiples of 15.*

## Set D — Geometric progressions

**D1.** (TITA) Find the 7th term of the GP 3, 6, 12, …

*Hint: §5 — aₙ = ar^(n−1). The exponent is n − 1, not n.*

**D2.** (MCQ) The sum of the infinite GP 8, 4, 2, 1, … is

- (a) 12
- (b) 15
- (c) 16
- (d) 18

*Hint: §5 — S∞ = a/(1 − r), valid because |r| < 1.*

**D3.** (TITA) Find the sum of the first 6 terms of the GP 2, 6, 18, …

*Hint: §5 — Sₙ = a(rⁿ − 1)/(r − 1) when r > 1.*

**D4.** (MCQ) In a GP the 3rd term is 12 and the 6th is 96. The first term is

- (a) 2
- (b) 3
- (c) 4
- (d) 6

*Hint: §5 — the ratio of the two given terms is r raised to the gap in positions.*

**D5.** (TITA) The sum of an infinite GP is 15 and the first term is 5. Find the common ratio (as a fraction).

*Hint: §5 — rearrange a/(1 − r) = S.*

**D6.** (MCQ) If the arithmetic mean of two numbers is 10 and their geometric mean is 8, the numbers are

- (a) 4 and 16
- (b) 5 and 15
- (c) 2 and 18
- (d) 6 and 14

*Hint: §5 — sum 20, product 64. They are the roots of a quadratic.*

## Set E — Harmonic progressions, means and standard sums

**E1.** (TITA) Find the harmonic mean of 4 and 12.

*Hint: §6 — HM = 2ab/(a + b). Not the average.*

**E2.** (MCQ) If the AM of two numbers is 25 and their HM is 16, their GM is

- (a) 18
- (b) 20
- (c) 21
- (d) 22.5

*Hint: §6 — GM² = AM × HM, the identity worth memorising.*

**E3.** (TITA) Find the sum 1² + 2² + … + 12².

*Hint: §4 — n(n + 1)(2n + 1)/6.*

**E4.** (MCQ) The value of 1³ + 2³ + … + 10³ is

- (a) 2025
- (b) 2500
- (c) 3025
- (d) 3125

*Hint: §4 — the sum of the first n cubes is the square of the sum of the first n naturals.*

**E5.** (TITA) The 4th term of an HP is 1/12 and the 8th is 1/20. Find the 12th term (as a fraction).

*Hint: §6 — take reciprocals to get an AP, work there, then invert at the very end.*

**M2.** (MCQ) The 15th term of the AP whose 3rd term is 10 and 7th term is 26 is

- (a) 50

- (c) 58

**M3.** (TITA) The sum of the first n terms of an AP is 3n² + 5n. Find its 10th term.

**M4.** (MCQ) The sum of the infinite series 1 + 1/3 + 1/9 + … is

- (a) 1.25
- (b) 1.5
- (c) 2

**M5.** (TITA) How many two-digit numbers leave a remainder of 3 when divided by 8?

**M6.** (MCQ) Three numbers in AP have sum 27 and product 504. The smallest is

- (a) 4
- (b) 6
- (c) 7

**M7.** (TITA) In a GP the 2nd term is 6 and the 5th is 162. Find the 8th term.

# Answer key

## Set A

**A1 — 83.** 7 + 19(4) = **83**.

**A2 — (c) 46.** (185 − 5)/4 + 1 = 45 + 1 = **46**.

**A3 — 5.** 4d = 29 − 17 = 12 ⟹ d = 3 ⟹ a = 17 − 4(3) = **5**.

**A4 — (c) 300.** a₄ + a₁₂ = 2a₈ = 40 ⟹ a₈ = 20 ⟹ S₁₅ = 15 × a₈ = **300**.

**A5 — 99.** 6d = 30 ⟹ d = 5 ⟹ a₂₀ = 34 + 13(5) = **99**.

## Set B

**B1 — 1830.** 15[6 + 116] = 15 × 122 = **1830**.

**B2 — (b) 728.** 14 to 98 with d = 7 ⟹ n = 13 ⟹ S = 13/2 (14 + 98) = 13 × 56 = **728**.

**B3 — 455.** S₂₀ = 10[4 + 57] = 610 and S₁₀ = 5[4 + 27] = 155 ⟹ difference = **455**.

**B4 — (c) 20.** 20 × 21 = 420 ⟹ n = **20**.

**B5 — 5.** 6[10 + 11d] = 390 ⟹ 10 + 11d = 65 ⟹ d = **5**.

**B6 — (b) 400.** 20² = **400**.

## Set C

**C1 — 50.** aₙ = 4n − 1 < 200 ⟹ n < 50.25 ⟹ **50 terms** (the 50th is 199).

**C2 — (c) 150.** (996 − 102)/6 + 1 = 149 + 1 = **150**.

**C3 — 25.** 108 to 396 with d = 12 ⟹ (396 − 108)/12 + 1 = **25**.

**C4 — (c) 16.** The first common term is 11, and the common difference is LCM(3, 4) = 12 ⟹ 11, 23, …, 191 ⟹ **16 terms**.

**C5 — 133.** Multiples of 3: 166 (3 to 498). Multiples of 15: 33 (15 to 495) ⟹ 166 − 33 = **133**.

## Set D

**D1 — 192.** 3 × 2⁶ = **192**.

**D2 — (c) 16.** 8/(1 − ½) = **16**.

**D3 — 728.** 2(3⁶ − 1)/2 = 729 − 1 = **728**.

**D4 — (b) 3.** r³ = 96/12 = 8 ⟹ r = 2 ⟹ a = 12/r² = **3**.

**D5 — 2/3.** 5/(1 − r) = 15 ⟹ 1 − r = 1/3 ⟹ r = **2/3**.

**D6 — (a) 4 and 16.** x² − 20x + 64 = 0 ⟹ **4 and 16**. (AM ≥ GM holds with 10 > 8 ✓)

## Set E

**E1 — 6.** 2(4)(12)/16 = **6**. (AM = 8, GM = √48 ≈ 6.93, HM = 6 — note AM ≥ GM ≥ HM ✓)

**E2 — (b) 20.** GM = √(25 × 16) = **20**.

**E3 — 650.** 12 × 13 × 25/6 = **650**.

**E4 — (c) 3025.** (55)² = **3025**.

**E5 — 1/28.** Reciprocals: a₄ = 12, a₈ = 20 ⟹ d = 2 ⟹ a₁₂ = 28 ⟹ the HP term is **1/28**.

## Mixed Set

**M1 — 55350.** 108 to 999 with d = 9 ⟹ n = 100 ⟹ S = 50(108 + 999) = **55,350**.

**M2 — (c) 58.** 4d = 16 ⟹ d = 4 ⟹ a₁₅ = 10 + 12(4) = **58**.

**M3 — 62.** S₁₀ = 350 and S₉ = 288 ⟹ a₁₀ = **62**.

**M4 — (b) 1.5.** 1/(1 − 1/3) = **1.5**.

**M5 — 12.** 11 to 99 with d = 8 ⟹ (99 − 11)/8 + 1 = **12**.

**M6 — (a) 4.** 3a = 27 ⟹ a = 9 ⟹ 9(81 − d²) = 504 ⟹ d² = 25 ⟹ the numbers are 4, 9, 14 ⟹ smallest = **4**.

**M7 — 4374.** r³ = 162/6 = 27 ⟹ r = 3 ⟹ a₈ = 162 × 27 = **4374**.

**M8 — (b) 14.** First common term 23, difference 28 ⟹ 23, 51, …, 387 (all ≤ 407) ⟹ **14 terms**.

**M9 — 40.** S₁₅ = 15 × a₈ ⟹ a₈ = **40**.

**M10 — (a) 8.** b² = ac = 64 ⟹ b = **8**. (17 is the arithmetic mean — the planted decoy.)

**Common error audit** — if you got a question wrong, find it here before moving on:

| Question | The error it is designed to catch |
|---|---|
| A1, A3, A5, D1, M2 | using n instead of n − 1 for the number of gaps (§1, §5) |
| A2, C2, C3, M5 | forgetting the +1 when counting terms in a range (§3) |
| A4, M9 | computing Sₙ the long way where n × (middle term) does it in one step (§4) |
| B3 | rebuilding a new AP for a term range instead of taking the difference of two sums (§2) |
| C3 | using the product of the divisors instead of their LCM |
| C4, M8 | guessing the first common term rather than finding it, or using the wrong upper limit |
| C5 | double-counting the multiples of 15 instead of subtracting them |
| D2, D5, M4 | using the finite-sum formula on an infinite GP, or forgetting the |r| < 1 condition (§5) |
| D4, M7 | taking the ratio of terms as r rather than as r raised to the position gap |
| E1, E2 | using the arithmetic mean where the harmonic or geometric mean is required (§6) |
| E5 | working directly in the HP instead of inverting to an AP first |
| M3 | solving for a and d where aₙ = Sₙ − Sₙ₋₁ is immediate |
| M6 | not centring the three terms on a, which makes the sum condition trivial |
| M10 | using the arithmetic mean for the middle term of a GP |
