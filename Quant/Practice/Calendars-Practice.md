# Calendars — Practice Sets

> Companion to `Calendars.md`. Hints point to the section of the notes that contains the intended method — read the hint only after you've been stuck for a minute. Answer key with short solutions is at the bottom.
>
> **TITA** = type-in-the-answer (no options). **MCQ** = choose one.
>
> Suggested timing: 1 min/question for Sets A–D, and attempt the Mixed Set in one timed block.
>
> **Everything is arithmetic modulo 7.** An "odd day" is the remainder left after removing whole weeks, and the whole topic is bookkeeping on those remainders (§1).
>
> The four anchors worth memorising: 100 years → 5 odd days, 200 → 3, 300 → 1, 400 → 0. A century year is a leap year only if divisible by 400.

## Set A — Odd days

**A1.** (TITA) How many odd days are there in 100 years?

*Hint: §2 — 100 years contain 24 leap years and 76 ordinary ones (1900-type century years are not leap).*

**A2.** (MCQ) The number of odd days in 400 years is

- (a) 0
- (b) 1
- (c) 3
- (d) 5

*Hint: §2 — the calendar repeats exactly every 400 years, which is precisely what this says.*

**A3.** (TITA) How many odd days are there in 300 years?

*Hint: §2 — three lots of 5.*

**A4.** (MCQ) A leap year contains how many odd days?

- (a) 1
- (b) 2
- (c) 3
- (d) 0

*Hint: §2 — 366 = 52 weeks + 2 days.*

**A5.** (TITA) How many odd days does February contribute in a leap year?

*Hint: §3 — 29 days is four weeks plus one.*

**A6.** (MCQ) The number of odd days from 1 January 2001 to 31 December 2010 is

- (a) 3
- (b) 4
- (c) 5
- (d) 6

*Hint: §2 — ten years, of which 2004 and 2008 are leap.*

## Set B — The day of the week for a given date

**B1.** (MCQ) 15 August 1947 was a

- (a) Thursday
- (b) Friday
- (c) Saturday
- (d) Sunday

*Hint: §4 — 1600 years (0) + 300 years (1) + 46 years + the days up to 15 August 1947.*

**B2.** (TITA) 1 January 2024 was a Monday. How many days after Monday is 1 January 2025? (Enter 0 for Monday, 1 for Tuesday, and so on.)

*Hint: §2 — 2024 is a leap year, so it contributes 2 odd days, not 1.*

**B3.** (MCQ) 26 January 1950 was a

- (a) Wednesday
- (b) Thursday
- (c) Friday
- (d) Saturday

*Hint: §4 — work from 1 January 1950, then add the 25 intervening days.*

**B4.** (TITA) 5 March 2023 was a Sunday. How many days after Sunday is 5 March 2024? (Enter 0 for Sunday.)

*Hint: §2 — the span contains 29 February 2024, so it is 366 days, not 365.*

**B5.** (MCQ) 1 January 2000 was a

- (a) Friday
- (b) Saturday
- (c) Sunday
- (d) Monday

*Hint: §4 — 1600 contributes 0 odd days, 300 years contribute 1, and 99 years contribute the rest.*

## Set C — Repeating calendars

**C1.** (MCQ) The calendar for 2007 was the same as the calendar for

- (a) 2016
- (b) 2017
- (c) 2018
- (d) 2019

*Hint: §5 — accumulate odd days year by year from 2007 until the running total is a multiple of 7.*

**C2.** (TITA) The calendar for 1993 is the same as that for which year?

*Hint: §5 — sum the odd days of 1993, 1994, … until they total 7.*

**C3.** (MCQ) A leap year's calendar repeats after

- (a) 6 years
- (b) 11 years
- (c) 28 years
- (d) 40 years

*Hint: §5 — a leap year has to line up with both the 7-day and the 4-year cycles.*

**C4.** (TITA) 2016 was a leap year. In which year will its calendar repeat?

*Hint: §5 — apply the 28-year rule; no century exception intervenes here.*

## Set D — Counting weekdays and days between dates

**D1.** (TITA) How many days are there from 1 March 2023 to 15 June 2023, both inclusive?

*Hint: §7 — add the whole months, then the partial one. Inclusive counting means the first day counts too.*

**D2.** (MCQ) The maximum number of Sundays that can occur in a 30-day month is

- (a) 4
- (b) 5
- (c) 6
- (d) 7

*Hint: §6 — 30 days is four weeks plus two, so at most two weekdays occur five times.*

**D3.** (TITA) How many Sundays are there in a non-leap year that begins on a Sunday?

*Hint: §6 — 365 days is 52 weeks plus one extra day, and that extra day is the one that repeats.*

**D4.** (MCQ) The number of days from 1 January 2024 to 31 December 2024, both inclusive, is

- (a) 364
- (b) 365
- (c) 366
- (d) 367

*Hint: §2 — 2024 is divisible by 4 and is not a century year.*

**D5.** (TITA) A 31-day month begins on a Friday. How many Fridays does it contain?

*Hint: §6 — 31 days is four weeks plus three, so the first three weekdays of the month occur five times.*

# Answer key

## Set A

**A1 — 5.** 76(1) + 24(2) = 124 ⟹ 124 mod 7 = **5 odd days**.

**A2 — (a) 0.** 5 + 5 + 5 + 6 = 21 ≡ **0** (mod 7). (The fourth century contributes 6 because its century year is a leap year.)

**A3 — 1.** 15 mod 7 = **1 odd day**.

**A4 — (b) 2.** 366 mod 7 = **2**.

**A5 — 1.** 29 mod 7 = **1**.

**A6 — (c) 5.** 8(1) + 2(2) = 12 ⟹ 12 mod 7 = **5**.

## Set B

**B1 — (b) Friday.** The odd days total 5 ⟹ **Friday**.

**B2 — 2 (Wednesday).** 366 mod 7 = 2 ⟹ 1 Jan 2025 is a Wednesday ⟹ **2**.

**B3 — (b) Thursday.** The odd days total 4 ⟹ **Thursday**.

**B4 — 2 (Tuesday).** 366 mod 7 = 2 ⟹ Tuesday ⟹ **2**. (Assuming 365 gives Monday — the planted error.)

**B5 — (b) Saturday.** The odd days total 6 ⟹ **Saturday**.

## Set C

**C1 — (c) 2018.** 2007–2012 contribute 1+1+2+1+1+1 … running to 7 by the end of 2017 ⟹ the calendar repeats in **2018**.

**C2 — 1999.** 1+1+1+2+1+1 = 7 by the end of 1998 ⟹ **1999**.

**C3 — (c) 28 years.** LCM-type reasoning gives **28 years** (away from century-year exceptions).

**C4 — 2044.** 2016 + 28 = **2044**.

## Set D

**D1 — 107.** 31 + 30 + 31 + 15 = **107 days**.

**D2 — (b) 5.** **5**.

**D3 — 53.** 52 weeks give 52 Sundays, and the extra day is also a Sunday ⟹ **53**.

**D4 — (c) 366.** **366**.

**D5 — 5.** Friday, Saturday and Sunday each occur five times ⟹ **5 Fridays**.

## Mixed Set

**M1 — (b) Thursday.** 1 Jan 2024 was a Monday; 2024 is leap (+2) ⟹ 1 Jan 2025 Wednesday; 2025 is ordinary (+1) ⟹ 1 Jan 2026 is a **Thursday**.

**M2 — 3.** 10 mod 7 = **3**.

**M3 — (b) Friday.** 100 mod 7 = 2 ⟹ **Friday**.

**M4 — 5 (Tuesday).** 366 + 365 + 365 + 365 = 1461 ⟹ 1461 mod 7 = 5 ⟹ Tuesday ⟹ **5**.

**M5 — (c) Wednesday.** Only Sunday, Tuesday, Thursday and Friday are possible ⟹ it cannot be a **Wednesday**.

**M6 — 53.** **53**.

**M7 — (c) 2011.** 1+1+1+2+1+1 = 7 by the end of 2010 ⟹ **2011**.

**M8 — 67.** (31 − 15 + 1) + 30 + 20 = 17 + 30 + 20 = **67 days**.

---

**Common error audit** — if you got a question wrong, find it here before moving on:

| Question | The error it is designed to catch |
|---|---|
| A1, A2, A3, M2 | forgetting that 1700, 1800 and 1900 are not leap years, which is what makes 100 years give 5 rather than 6 (§2) |
| A4, B2, B4 | counting a leap year as 1 odd day, or missing that a March-to-March span crosses 29 February (§2) |
| A6 | counting the leap years in the wrong window |
| B1, B3, B5 | mishandling the century anchor, or forgetting the days elapsed within the final year (§4) |
| C1, C2, C3, C4, M7 | adding a fixed 6 or 11 years instead of accumulating odd days to a multiple of 7 (§5) |
| D1, M8 | off-by-one from inclusive counting — the first day counts (§7) |
| D2, D3, D5, M6 | assuming every weekday occurs the same number of times; the surplus days decide which repeat (§6) |
| M4 | counting the wrong number of 29 Februarys inside the span |
| M5 | not knowing that only four weekdays are possible for the last day of a century |
