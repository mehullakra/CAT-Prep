# Profit, Loss & Discount — Practice Sets

> Companion to `Profit-Loss-Discount.md`. Hints point to the section of the notes that contains the intended method — read the hint only after you've been stuck for a minute. Answer key with short solutions is at the bottom.
>
> **TITA** = type-in-the-answer (no options). **MCQ** = choose one.
>
> Suggested timing: 1.5 min/question for Sets A–F, and attempt the Mixed Set in one timed block.
>
> **Profit % is always on CP; discount % is always on MP.** Half the errors in this topic are a correct percentage applied to the wrong base (§2).
>
> Chain everything as factors: markup, discount and wastage each multiply, and they act on different quantities (§12).

---

## Set A — Cost price, selling price and profit percent

**A1.** (TITA) An article bought for ₹640 is sold for ₹768. Find the profit percent.

*Hint: §1 — profit % is on CP. 128/640.*

**A2.** (MCQ) An article sold for ₹450 gives a 25% profit. Its cost price is

- (a) ₹337.50
- (b) ₹360
- (c) ₹375
- (d) ₹400

*Hint: §3 — divide by 1.25. Subtracting 25% of the SP is the planted error.*

**A3.** (TITA) A man sells an article at a 15% loss for ₹1,020. Find the cost price (in ₹).

*Hint: §3 — a 15% loss means SP = 0.85 × CP.*

**A4.** (MCQ) If the cost price of 20 articles equals the selling price of 16 articles, the profit percent is

- (a) 20%
- (b) 25%
- (c) 30%
- (d) 33.33%

*Hint: §9 — the larger number sits on the CP side, so it's a profit. SP/CP = 20/16.*

**A5.** (TITA) By selling an article for ₹900 a man loses 10%. At what price should he sell it to gain 20% (in ₹)?

*Hint: §1 — recover CP first. Every profit or loss percentage refers back to that one number.*

**A6.** (MCQ) A trader's profit is 25% of the cost price. His profit as a percentage of the selling price is

- (a) 16.67%
- (b) 20%
- (c) 25%
- (d) 33.33%

*Hint: §2 — the base changes from CP to SP. Take CP = 100.*

## Set B — Marked price and a single discount

**B1.** (TITA) An article marked at ₹1,500 is sold at a 12% discount. Find the selling price (in ₹).

*Hint: §4 — discount is on MP, always.*

**B2.** (MCQ) A shopkeeper marks his goods 40% above cost and allows a 25% discount. His profit percent is

- (a) 5%
- (b) 10%
- (c) 15%
- (d) 20%

*Hint: §4 — chain 1.4 and 0.75. The markup and the discount act on different bases, so they never simply subtract.*

**B3.** (TITA) A shopkeeper wants a 20% profit after allowing a 20% discount. By what percent above cost must he mark his goods?

*Hint: §4 read backwards — markup factor = (profit factor)/(discount factor).*

**B4.** (MCQ) An article marked at ₹800 is sold for ₹680. The rate of discount is

- (a) 12%
- (b) 15%
- (c) 17.65%
- (d) 20%

*Hint: §4 — divide the discount by the MP, not by the SP. One option is the SP-based answer.*

**B5.** (TITA) A shopkeeper marks an article 30% above cost and gives a discount, ending with a 4% profit. Find the discount percent.

*Hint: §4 — the discount factor is (profit factor)/(markup factor).*

## Set C — Successive discounts

**C1.** (MCQ) Successive discounts of 20% and 10% are equivalent to a single discount of

- (a) 26%
- (b) 28%
- (c) 30%
- (d) 32%

*Hint: §5 — 0.8 × 0.9. The naive sum 30% is planted.*

**C2.** (TITA) An article marked at ₹2,500 is sold after successive discounts of 20%, 10% and 5%. Find the selling price (in ₹).

*Hint: §5 — chain all three factors, then multiply once.*

**C3.** (MCQ) Which is the better deal for a buyer: a single discount of 30%, or successive discounts of 20% and 10%?

- (a) The single 30% discount
- (b) The successive discounts
- (c) They are identical
- (d) Cannot be determined

*Hint: §5 — compare the surviving factors 0.70 and 0.72. Smaller is better for the buyer.*

**C4.** (TITA) Successive discounts of 25% and x% are equivalent to a single discount of 40%. Find x.

*Hint: §5 — set 0.75 × (1 − x/100) = 0.60 and solve for the second factor.*

**C5.** (MCQ) After two equal successive discounts, an article marked at ₹10,000 sells for ₹8,100. Each discount is

- (a) 9%
- (b) 9.5%
- (c) 10%
- (d) 19%

*Hint: §5 — 0.81 is a perfect square. Take the square root of the surviving factor.*

## Set D — The two classic traps

**D1.** (MCQ) A man sells two articles at ₹960 each, gaining 20% on one and losing 20% on the other. Overall he

- (a) gains 4%
- (b) loses 4%
- (c) breaks even
- (d) loses 2%

*Hint: §6 — same SP with equal ± percentages is always a loss of x²/100 percent. Never a break-even.*

**D2.** (TITA) A man sells two horses at ₹4,000 each, gaining 25% on one and losing 25% on the other. Find his net loss (in ₹), to two decimals.

*Hint: §6 — the percentage loss is 25²/100 = 6.25%, but the question asks for the rupee amount, so find both CPs.*

**D3.** (MCQ) An article is sold at a 20% profit. Had it been bought for 20% less and sold for ₹90 less, the profit would have been 25%. The original cost price was

- (a) ₹400
- (b) ₹425
- (c) ₹450
- (d) ₹500

*Hint: §6 — write both scenarios in terms of the same C and equate. Only one unknown is needed.*

**D4.** (TITA) By selling an article at ₹720 a man loses as much as he would gain by selling it at ₹880. Find the cost price (in ₹).

*Hint: §6 — equal rupee loss and gain means the CP is exactly midway.*

**D5.** (MCQ) If the selling price of 10 articles equals the cost price of 12 articles, the profit percent is

- (a) 16.67%
- (b) 20%
- (c) 25%
- (d) 30%

*Hint: §9 — here the larger number is on the CP side, so it is a profit. SP/CP = 12/10.*

## Set E — False weights and adulteration

**E1.** (TITA) A shopkeeper sells at cost price but uses a 900 g weight for 1 kg. Find his profit percent, to two decimals.

*Hint: §7 — the base is what he actually gives, not what he claims. 100/900, not 100/1000.*

**E2.** (MCQ) A dealer professes to sell at cost price but uses an 800 g weight for a kilogram. His gain is

- (a) 20%
- (b) 22.5%
- (c) 25%
- (d) 33.33%

*Hint: §7 — 200/800.*

**E3.** (TITA) A shopkeeper uses a 950 g weight for 1 kg and also marks up his goods 20%. Find his overall profit percent, to two decimals.

*Hint: §7 + §12 — the markup and the short weight are independent factors, so multiply them.*

**E4.** (MCQ) Milk costing ₹40/litre is mixed with water and the mixture sold at ₹40/litre, yielding a 25% profit. The ratio of water to milk in the mixture is

- (a) 1 : 3
- (b) 1 : 4
- (c) 1 : 5
- (d) 2 : 5

*Hint: §8 — the base is the pure quantity, not the mixture. Water is free, so profit % = water/milk.*

**E5.** (TITA) A milkman mixes water with milk and sells the mixture at cost price, making a 20% profit. Find the percentage of water in the mixture, to two decimals.

*Hint: §8 — 20% profit gives water : milk = 1 : 5, but the question asks for water as a share of the mixture.*

## Set F — Equating goods, bulk offers and combined chains

**F1.** (MCQ) "Buy 3 get 1 free" is equivalent to a discount of

- (a) 25%
- (b) 33.33%
- (c) 20%
- (d) 30%

*Hint: §10 — the base is what you receive (4 items), not what you pay for (3).*

**F2.** (TITA) A shop offers "buy 4 get 2 free". Find the equivalent discount percent, to two decimals.

*Hint: §10 — 2 free out of the 6 you take home.*

**F3.** (MCQ) If the cost price of 15 articles equals the selling price of 12 articles, then there is a

- (a) 20% loss
- (b) 20% profit
- (c) 25% profit
- (d) 25% loss

*Hint: §9 — larger number on the CP side means a profit. Then SP/CP = 15/12.*

**F4.** (TITA) A shopkeeper marks his goods 50% above cost, allows a 20% discount, and finds 10% of his stock spoils and cannot be sold. Find his overall profit percent.

*Hint: §12 — markup and discount act on the price; wastage acts on the quantity. Assume 100 units at ₹1 each.*

**F5.** (MCQ) A trader sells two items at the same price, one at a 25% profit and the other at a 25% loss, with an overall loss of ₹500. His total selling price was

- (a) ₹6,000
- (b) ₹7,000
- (c) ₹7,500
- (d) ₹8,000

*Hint: §6 + §11 — the loss is 6.25% of the total CP, and the total CP is expressible in terms of the common SP.*

## Mixed Set — exam feel (15 minutes)

**M1.** (MCQ) A shopkeeper marks goods 25% above cost and allows a 10% discount. His profit percent is

- (a) 10%
- (b) 12.5%
- (c) 15%
- (d) 15.5%

**M2.** (TITA) An article costing ₹2,400 is sold at a 15% loss. Find the selling price (in ₹).

**M3.** (MCQ) Successive discounts of 10%, 20% and 25% are equivalent to a single discount of

- (a) 45%
- (b) 46%
- (c) 50%
- (d) 55%

**M4.** (TITA) A dishonest trader sells at cost price but gives 750 g for 1 kg. Find his profit percent, to two decimals.

**M5.** (MCQ) A man buys 60 apples for ₹300 and sells them at 5 for ₹30. His profit percent is

- (a) 15%
- (b) 20%
- (c) 25%
- (d) 30%

**M6.** (TITA) A shopkeeper wants a 25% profit after giving a 20% discount on the marked price. By what percent above cost must he mark the goods?

**M7.** (MCQ) Two articles are sold at ₹1,200 each, one at a 20% profit and the other at a 20% loss. The net result is

- (a) a loss of ₹100
- (b) a gain of ₹100
- (c) no profit no loss
- (d) a loss of ₹200

**M8.** (TITA) By selling 45 lemons for ₹40 a man loses 20%. How many lemons must he sell for ₹24 to gain 20%?

**M9.** (MCQ) The marked price of an article is 20% above its cost price. After a discount the trader still makes a 2% profit. The discount is

- (a) 12%
- (b) 15%
- (c) 18%
- (d) 20%

**M10.** (TITA) A man buys milk at ₹30/litre, adds water, and sells the mixture at ₹36/litre for a 50% profit. Find the litres of water added per litre of milk.

# Answer key

## Set A

**A1 — 20%.** Profit = 128 ⟹ 128/640 = **20%**.

**A2 — (b) ₹360.** 450/1.25 = **₹360**. (450 × 0.75 = ₹337.50 is the decoy.)

**A3 — ₹1,200.** 1020/0.85 = **₹1,200**.

**A4 — (b) 25%.** 20 CP = 16 SP ⟹ SP/CP = 5/4 ⟹ profit = **25%**.

**A5 — ₹1,200.** CP = 900/0.9 = ₹1,000 ⟹ SP for 20% gain = **₹1,200**.

**A6 — (b) 20%.** CP 100, profit 25, SP 125 ⟹ 25/125 = **20%**.

## Set B

**B1 — ₹1,320.** 1500 × 0.88 = **₹1,320**.

**B2 — (a) 5%.** 1.4 × 0.75 = 1.05 ⟹ **5%** profit. (40 − 25 = 15% is the planted error.)

**B3 — 50%.** 1.2/0.8 = 1.5 ⟹ mark up by **50%**. (Answering 40% assumes the two 20%s cancel.)

**B4 — (b) 15%.** 120/800 = **15%**. (120/680 = 17.65% uses the wrong base.)

**B5 — 20%.** 1.04/1.3 = 0.8 ⟹ discount = **20%**.

## Set C

**C1 — (b) 28%.** 0.8 × 0.9 = 0.72 ⟹ a single discount of **28%**.

**C2 — ₹1,710.** 2500 × 0.8 × 0.9 × 0.95 = 2500 × 0.684 = **₹1,710**.

**C3 — (a) The single 30% discount.** 0.70 vs 0.8 × 0.9 = 0.72 ⟹ **the single 30% discount** is better. Successive discounts always undershoot their sum.

**C4 — 20%.** 0.75(1 − x/100) = 0.60 ⟹ 1 − x/100 = 0.8 ⟹ x = **20%**. (40 − 25 = 15% is the planted error.)

**C5 — (c) 10%.** (1 − d)² = 0.81 ⟹ 1 − d = 0.9 ⟹ each discount is **10%**. (19% is the single equivalent discount, planted.)

## Set D

**D1 — (b) loses 4%.** Loss = 20²/100 = **4%**. (CPs are ₹800 and ₹1,200, total ₹2,000 against an SP of ₹1,920.)

**D2 — ₹533.33.** CPs = 4000/1.25 = ₹3,200 and 4000/0.75 = ₹5,333.33 ⟹ total CP ₹8,533.33 against SP ₹8,000 ⟹ loss = **₹533.33** (6.25% of CP ✓).

**D3 — (c) ₹450.** 1.2C − 90 = 1.25(0.8C) = C ⟹ 0.2C = 90 ⟹ C = **₹450**. Check: SP 540 vs new CP 360 and new SP 450 = 25% profit ✓

**D4 — ₹800.** CP = (720 + 880)/2 = **₹800**.

**D5 — (b) 20%.** 10 SP = 12 CP ⟹ SP/CP = 1.2 ⟹ profit = **20%**. (Reading it as 10/12 gives a 16.67% loss — the direction trap.)

## Set E

**E1 — 11.11%.** He gains 100 g on a true cost of 900 g ⟹ 100/900 = **11.11%**. (10% uses the wrong base.)

**E2 — (c) 25%.** 200/800 = **25%**. (20% would be 200/1000 — the wrong base.)

**E3 — 26.32%.** 1.2 × (1000/950) = 1.2 × 1.05263 = 1.26316 ⟹ **26.32%**. (Adding 20 + 5.26 gives 25.26% — close enough to be a plausible wrong answer.)

**E4 — (b) 1 : 4.** Profit 25% = 1/4 ⟹ water : milk = **1 : 4**. Check: 4 L milk costs ₹160, 5 L sells for ₹200, profit 40/160 = 25% ✓

**E5 — 16.67%.** Water : milk = 1 : 5 ⟹ water as a fraction of the 6-part mixture = 1/6 = **16.67%**. (Answering 20% confuses the two bases.)

## Set F

**F1 — (a) 25%.** You get 4 for the price of 3 ⟹ 1/4 = **25%**. (33.33% uses 3 as the base — the planted error.)

**F2 — 33.33%.** 2/6 = **33.33%**.

**F3 — (c) 25% profit.** SP/CP = 15/12 = 1.25 ⟹ a **25% profit**.

**F4 — 8%.** Cost = ₹100. He sells 90 units at 1.5 × 0.8 = ₹1.20 each = ₹108 ⟹ profit = **8%**.

**F5 — (c) ₹7,500.** With each SP = x: CPs are 0.8x and (4/3)x ⟹ total CP = (32/15)x against total SP = 2x ⟹ loss = (2/15)x = 500 ⟹ x = ₹3,750 ⟹ total SP = **₹7,500**.

## Mixed Set

**M1 — (b) 12.5%.** 1.25 × 0.9 = 1.125 ⟹ **12.5%**.

**M2 — ₹2,040.** 2400 × 0.85 = **₹2,040**.

**M3 — (b) 46%.** 0.9 × 0.8 × 0.75 = 0.54 ⟹ **46%**. (The naive sum 55% is planted.)

**M4 — 33.33%.** 250/750 = **33.33%**.

**M5 — (b) 20%.** SP = 12 × 30 = ₹360 against CP ₹300 ⟹ **20%**.

**M6 — 56.25%.** 1.25/0.8 = 1.5625 ⟹ mark up by **56.25%**.

**M7 — (a) a loss of ₹100.** CPs = ₹1,000 and ₹1,500 ⟹ total CP ₹2,500 against SP ₹2,400 ⟹ **a loss of ₹100** (4% ✓).

**M8 — 18.** SP per lemon = 40/45 = 8/9 ⟹ CP = (8/9)/0.8 = 10/9. For a 20% gain, SP = 1.2 × 10/9 = 4/3 ⟹ 24 ÷ 4/3 = **18 lemons**.

**M9 — (b) 15%.** 1.02/1.2 = 0.85 ⟹ discount = **15%**.

**M10 — 0.25.** Per litre of milk: cost ₹30, required revenue ₹45 ⟹ mixture volume = 45/36 = 1.25 L ⟹ water = **0.25 litres**.

**Common error audit** — if you got a question wrong, find it here before moving on:

| Question | The error it is designed to catch |
|---|---|
| A2, A3, A6, B4 | applying a percentage to the wrong base — profit is on CP, discount is on MP (§2) |
| A4, D5, F3 | getting the direction wrong when equating goods; the larger number's side decides profit vs loss (§9) |
| B2, B3, B5, M1, M6, M9 | treating markup and discount as cancelling because the percentages match — the bases differ (§4) |
| C1, C2, C4, M3 | adding successive discounts instead of chaining the factors (§5) |
| C5 | reporting the combined discount where each individual one was asked |
| D1, D2, M7 | assuming equal ± percentages on the same SP break even; it is always an x²/100 loss (§6) |
| D3 | introducing two unknowns where one C carries both scenarios |
| E1, E2, M4 | dividing the short weight's gain by 1000 instead of by what was actually given (§7) |
| E4, E5 | using the mixture as the base where the pure quantity is the base, or vice versa (§8) |
| F1, F2 | using the number paid for as the base instead of the number received (§10) |
| F4 | applying wastage to the price rather than to the quantity sold (§12) |
| M8, M10 | abandoning per-unit working, which is what keeps these two tractable |
