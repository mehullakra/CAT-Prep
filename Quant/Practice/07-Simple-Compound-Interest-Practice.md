# Simple & Compound Interest — Practice Sets

> Companion to `07-Simple-Compound-Interest.md`. Hints point to the section of the notes that contains the intended method — read the hint only after you've been stuck for a minute. Answer key with short solutions is at the bottom.
>
> **TITA** = type-in-the-answer (no options). **MCQ** = choose one.
>
> Suggested timing: 1.5 min/question for Sets A–H, and attempt the Mixed Set in one timed block.
>
> **Before computing anything, write down whether the question wants the interest or the amount.** That single misread costs more marks in this topic than every formula error combined (§2).
>
> For CI, always work in growth factors — 12.5% is ×9/8, not "a formula".

---

## Set A — Simple interest fundamentals

**A1.** (TITA) Find the simple interest on ₹7,200 at 8.5% p.a. for 3 years (in ₹).

*Hint: §1 — SI = Prn/100. One line, no traps.*

**A2.** (MCQ) A sum of ₹6,000 amounts to ₹7,440 in 4 years under SI. The rate per annum is

- (a) 5%
- (b) 6%
- (c) 7.5%
- (d) 8%

*Hint: §2 — the ₹7,440 is the amount, not the interest. Subtract first.*

**A3.** (TITA) At what rate percent per annum will ₹1,200 yield ₹216 as simple interest in 2 years and 3 months?

*Hint: Traps table — convert 3 months to a fraction of a year before touching the formula.*

**A4.** (MCQ) A sum triples itself in 12 years under simple interest. The rate per annum is

- (a) 12.5%
- (b) 16.67%
- (c) 20%
- (d) 25%

*Hint: §6 — tripling means the interest earned equals 2P, not 3P. Then rt = 200.*

**A5.** (TITA) The SI on a sum for 4 years at 6% p.a. exceeds the SI on the same sum for 3 years at 5% p.a. by ₹540. Find the sum (in ₹).

*Hint: §1 — both interests are linear in P, so the difference is P times a single number.*

**A6.** (MCQ) ₹800 becomes ₹956 in 3 years under SI. If the rate is increased by 4 percentage points, the same sum over the same period becomes

- (a) ₹1,020
- (b) ₹1,052
- (c) ₹1,076
- (d) ₹1,112

*Hint: §1 — find the original rate first. Under SI, 4 extra percentage points for 3 years adds exactly 12% of P.*

## Set B — Compound interest and the factor form

**B1.** (TITA) Find the compound interest on ₹12,500 at 8% p.a. for 2 years (in ₹).

*Hint: §1 — multiply by 1.08 twice, then subtract P. The question asks for interest.*

**B2.** (MCQ) ₹6,250 at 4% p.a. compounded annually for 2 years amounts to

- (a) ₹6,720
- (b) ₹6,750
- (c) ₹6,760
- (d) ₹6,800

*Hint: §1 — 1.04² = 1.0816. Note that ₹6,750 is the SI answer, planted as a decoy.*

**B3.** (TITA) Find the compound interest on ₹16,000 at 12.5% p.a. for 2 years (in ₹).

*Hint: Practical exam habits — 12.5% is the growth factor 9/8. Two multiplications by 9/8 beat any decimal.*

**B4.** (MCQ) The CI on a sum for 2 years at 10% p.a. is ₹2,100. The SI on the same sum for the same period is

- (a) ₹1,900
- (b) ₹2,000
- (c) ₹2,050
- (d) ₹2,110

*Hint: §1 — recover P from the CI factor (1.21 − 1), then apply SI. Or use §4 backwards.*

**B5.** (TITA) A sum amounts to ₹9,261 in 3 years at 5% p.a. CI. Find the sum (in ₹).

*Hint: §2 — divide by the factor, don't subtract. 9261 is a cube, which is the hint CAT is giving you.*

**B6.** (TITA) ₹25,000 is invested at 4% in the first year, 5% in the second and 10% in the third, compounded annually. Find the amount at the end of 3 years (in ₹).

*Hint: §8 — just chain the three factors. Do not average the rates.*

## Set C — Compounding more than once a year

**C1.** (TITA) Find the amount on ₹8,000 at 10% p.a. compounded half-yearly for 1 year (in ₹).

*Hint: §3 — rate per period 5%, number of periods 2. The rate halves and the periods double.*

**C2.** (MCQ) ₹16,000 at 20% p.a. compounded quarterly for 9 months amounts to

- (a) ₹18,150
- (b) ₹18,522
- (c) ₹18,816
- (d) ₹19,200

*Hint: §3 — 9 months is 3 quarters, so 5% for 3 periods.*

**C3.** (TITA) Find the compound interest on ₹10,000 at 4% p.a. compounded half-yearly for 1½ years (in ₹).

*Hint: §3 — 2% for 3 periods. Then §2: subtract the principal.*

**C4.** (MCQ) ₹4,000 is invested for 1 year at 10% p.a. The amount under half-yearly compounding exceeds the amount under annual compounding by

- (a) ₹10
- (b) ₹20
- (c) ₹25
- (d) ₹40

*Hint: §3 — you know the sign before computing: more frequent compounding always wins. Compute both amounts and subtract.*

**C5.** (TITA) At what rate percent per annum, compounded half-yearly, will ₹6,400 amount to ₹6,724 in 1 year?

*Hint: §3 read backwards — 6724/6400 is a perfect square of the half-yearly factor. Double the half-yearly rate at the end.*

## Set D — The CI − SI gap

**D1.** (TITA) The difference between CI and SI on a sum for 2 years at 8% p.a. is ₹64. Find the sum (in ₹).

*Hint: §4 — CI − SI = P(r/100)² for 2 years. One division.*

**D2.** (MCQ) The difference between CI and SI on ₹15,000 for 2 years at 6% p.a. is

- (a) ₹36
- (b) ₹54
- (c) ₹90
- (d) ₹108

*Hint: §4 — the same formula forwards.*

**D3.** (TITA) The difference between CI and SI on a sum for 3 years at 10% p.a. is ₹93. Find the sum (in ₹).

*Hint: §4 — the 3-year formula Pr²(300 + r)/10⁶. Using the 2-year formula here is the planted error.*

**D4.** (MCQ) The SI on a sum for 2 years at 12% p.a. is ₹1,200. The CI on the same sum, same rate, same period is

- (a) ₹1,236
- (b) ₹1,260
- (c) ₹1,272
- (d) ₹1,344

*Hint: §4 — find P from the SI, then add P(r/100)² to ₹1,200. Sanity check: CI must exceed SI.*

**D5.** (TITA) For a certain sum, the difference between CI and SI over 2 years is ₹40, and the SI for 1 year is ₹500. Find the rate percent per annum.

*Hint: §4, the "also useful" line — CI − SI over 2 years = (SI for 1 year) × r/100. P never appears.*

**D6.** (MCQ) ₹20,000 is lent for 3 years at 5% p.a. The difference between CI (compounded annually) and SI is

- (a) ₹50
- (b) ₹125
- (c) ₹152.50
- (d) ₹200

*Hint: §4, 3-year formula. One of the options is what you get from the 2-year formula — that's the trap.*

## Set E — Finding rate, time or principal from amounts

**E1.** (TITA) Under CI a sum amounts to ₹2,420 in 2 years and ₹2,662 in 3 years. Find the rate percent per annum.

*Hint: §5 — consecutive years, so the ratio of the amounts is the growth factor itself.*

**E2.** (TITA) A sum amounts to ₹4,840 in 2 years and ₹5,324 in 3 years under CI. Find the principal (in ₹).

*Hint: §5 — get r from the ratio, then divide the 2-year amount by the factor squared.*

**E3.** (MCQ) Under SI a sum amounts to ₹2,240 in 3 years and ₹2,600 in 6 years. The principal is

- (a) ₹1,760
- (b) ₹1,820
- (c) ₹1,880
- (d) ₹1,920

*Hint: §5, SI case — the yearly interest is constant, so take the difference over the gap in years and work backwards.*

**E4.** (TITA) Under CI, the interest earned in the second year is ₹660 and in the third year is ₹726. Find the rate percent per annum.

*Hint: §4, last line — consecutive years' CI are in the ratio of the growth factor.*

**E5.** (MCQ) In how many years will ₹5,000 amount to ₹6,655 at 10% p.a. compounded annually?

- (a) 2
- (b) 3
- (c) 4
- (d) 5

*Hint: §1 — divide, then recognise the power of 1.1. Don't take logs.*

**E6.** (TITA) At what rate percent per annum CI will ₹4,096 amount to ₹4,913 in 3 years?

*Hint: Practical exam habits — both numbers are perfect cubes. That is the whole question.*

## Set F — Doubling and multiplying

**F1.** (MCQ) A sum doubles in 8 years under CI. In how many years does it become 16 times?

- (a) 24
- (b) 32
- (c) 48
- (d) 64

*Hint: §6 — CI multiplies. Write 16 as a power of 2.*

**F2.** (TITA) A sum doubles in 4 years under SI. In how many years does it become 6 times?

*Hint: §6 — SI adds. Doubling in 4 years means the sum earns P of interest every 4 years.*

**F3.** (MCQ) A sum becomes 3 times in 6 years at CI. In how many years does it become 81 times?

- (a) 18
- (b) 24
- (c) 30
- (d) 36

*Hint: §6 — 81 = 3⁴.*

**F4.** (TITA) At what rate of simple interest will a sum double itself in 16 years?

*Hint: §6 — doubling under SI means rt = 100.*

**F5.** (MCQ) A sum becomes 8 times in 12 years under CI. It becomes 2 times in

- (a) 3 years
- (b) 4 years
- (c) 6 years
- (d) 9 years

*Hint: §6 run backwards — 8 = 2³, so 12 years is three doubling periods.*

**F6.** (TITA) Under SI a sum becomes 5 times in 20 years. In how many years does it become 9 times at the same rate?

*Hint: §6 — 5 times means interest of 4P. Find how long one P of interest takes, then scale.*

## Set G — Instalments

**G1.** (TITA) A loan of ₹2,550 is repaid in 2 equal annual instalments at 4% p.a. CI. Find each instalment (in ₹).

*Hint: §7 — discount each instalment back, or use the shortcut X = P(1 + r)²/(2 + r).*

**G2.** (MCQ) ₹820 borrowed at 5% p.a. CI is repaid in 2 equal annual instalments. Each instalment is

- (a) ₹420
- (b) ₹430.50
- (c) ₹441
- (d) ₹451

*Hint: §7 — the two instalments are discounted by 1.05 and 1.05², not by 1.05 both times.*

**G3.** (TITA) A sum borrowed at 10% p.a. CI is repaid in 3 equal annual instalments of ₹1,331 each. Find the sum borrowed (in ₹).

*Hint: §7 — discount 1331 by 1.1, 1.1² and 1.1³ and add. The numbers are chosen to come out whole.*

**G4.** (MCQ) Three equal annual instalments, each paid at the end of a year, clear a debt at 12% p.a. simple interest. If the accumulated value of the three instalments at the end of year 3 is ₹1,092, each instalment is

- (a) ₹300
- (b) ₹325
- (c) ₹350
- (d) ₹364

*Hint: §7, SI version — accumulate forward instead of discounting back. The first instalment earns SI for 2 years, the second for 1, the third for none.*

**G5.** (TITA) A man borrows ₹6,000 at 5% p.a. CI and repays ₹2,300 at the end of the first year. What must he pay at the end of the second year to clear the debt (in ₹)?

*Hint: §7 — grow, subtract the payment, grow again. Order matters: interest accrues before the payment lands.*

## Set H — Depreciation, population and split principals

**H1.** (TITA) A machine worth ₹6,25,000 depreciates at 20% p.a. Find its value after 3 years (in ₹).

*Hint: §9 — the factor is 0.8, and 0.8³ = 0.512.*

**H2.** (MCQ) The population of a town is 1,05,800 now and has been growing at 15% p.a. Its population 2 years ago was

- (a) 76,000
- (b) 80,000
- (c) 84,000
- (d) 92,000

*Hint: §9 — "n years ago" means divide by the factor, not subtract 15% twice.*

**H3.** (TITA) A village population rises 10% in the first year and falls 10% in the second. If the final population is 29,700, find the original population.

*Hint: §9, mixed direction — 1.1 × 0.9 = 0.99, not 1.*

**H4.** (MCQ) ₹15,000 is split between two schemes offering 10% and 15% simple interest. If the total interest in one year is ₹1,950, the amount at 15% is

- (a) ₹6,000
- (b) ₹7,500
- (c) ₹9,000
- (d) ₹10,000

*Hint: §10 — find the overall rate first, then apply alligation. Watch which side of the cross belongs to which scheme.*

**H5.** (TITA) ₹8,000 is invested partly at 6% and partly at 10% SI. The total interest in one year is ₹640. Find the amount invested at 10% (in ₹).

*Hint: §10 — the overall rate sits exactly halfway, so no arithmetic is needed after that observation.*

**H6.** (MCQ) A car depreciates 10% in the first year and 20% in the second. If it is worth ₹2,88,000 after two years, its original price was

- (a) ₹3,60,000
- (b) ₹4,00,000
- (c) ₹4,20,000
- (d) ₹4,50,000

*Hint: §8 + §9 — chain 0.9 × 0.8, then divide.*

# Answer key

## Set A

**A1 — ₹1,836.** 7200 × 8.5 × 3/100. Take 8.5% of 7200 = 612, then ×3 = **₹1,836**.

**A2 — (b) 6%.** SI = 7440 − 6000 = 1440 over 4 years ⟹ ₹360/year ⟹ r = 360/6000 × 100 = **6%**.

**A3 — 8%.** t = 2¼ = 9/4 years. 1200 × r × (9/4)/100 = 216 ⟹ 27r = 216 ⟹ r = **8%**.

**A4 — (b) 16.67%.** Tripling ⟹ interest = 2P ⟹ rt = 200 ⟹ r = 200/12 = **16.67%**. (Using rt = 300 gives 25% — the trap.)

**A5 — ₹6,000.** P(4 × 0.06 − 3 × 0.05) = 540 ⟹ 0.09P = 540 ⟹ P = **₹6,000**.

**A6 — (b) ₹1,052.** SI = 156 over 3 years ⟹ r = 156/(800 × 3) × 100 = 6.5%. New rate 10.5% ⟹ SI = 800 × 0.105 × 3 = 252 ⟹ amount = **₹1,052**. Shortcut: 4 extra points for 3 years adds 12% of 800 = ₹96, and 956 + 96 = 1052 ✓

## Set B

**B1 — ₹2,080.** 12500 × 1.08² = 12500 × 1.1664 = 14,580 ⟹ CI = **₹2,080**. (₹14,580 is the amount — read the question.)

**B2 — (c) ₹6,760.** 6250 × 1.0816 = **₹6,760**. (SI would give 6250 + 500 = ₹6,750.)

**B3 — ₹4,250.** Factor 9/8: 16000 × 9/8 = 18,000; 18000 × 9/8 = 20,250 ⟹ CI = **₹4,250**.

**B4 — (b) ₹2,000.** P(1.21 − 1) = 2100 ⟹ P = 10,000. SI = 10000 × 0.10 × 2 = **₹2,000**. Check with §4: CI − SI = 10000(0.1)² = 100, and 2100 − 2000 = 100 ✓

**B5 — ₹8,000.** 1.05³ = 1.157625 ⟹ P = 9261/1.157625 = **₹8,000**. Spotting 9261 = 21³ and 1.05³ = (21/20)³ makes this instant: P = 20³ = 8000.

**B6 — ₹30,030.** 25000 × 1.04 = 26,000; × 1.05 = 27,300; × 1.10 = **₹30,030**. Order is irrelevant — the factors commute.

## Set C

**C1 — ₹8,820.** 8000 × 1.05² = 8000 × 1.1025 = **₹8,820**.

**C2 — (b) ₹18,522.** 16000 × 1.05³ = 16000 × 1.157625 = **₹18,522**.

**C3 — ₹612.08.** 10000 × 1.02³ = 10,612.08 ⟹ CI = **₹612.08**.

**C4 — (a) ₹10.** Half-yearly: 4000 × 1.05² = ₹4,410. Annual: 4000 × 1.1 = ₹4,400. Difference = **₹10**. Equivalently by §4, the gap is 4000 × 0.05² = 10.

**C5 — 5%.** 6724/6400 = 1.050625 = 1.025² ⟹ half-yearly rate 2.5% ⟹ annual rate = **5%**. (Answering 2.5% is the trap: the question asks per annum.)

## Set D

**D1 — ₹10,000.** P(0.08)² = 64 ⟹ 0.0064P = 64 ⟹ P = **₹10,000**.

**D2 — (b) ₹54.** 15000 × (0.06)² = 15000 × 0.0036 = **₹54**.

**D3 — ₹3,000.** P × 100 × 310/10⁶ = 0.031P = 93 ⟹ P = **₹3,000**. (The 2-year formula would give ₹9,300 — wrong.)

**D4 — (c) ₹1,272.** SI 1200 over 2 years ⟹ ₹600/year ⟹ P = ₹5,000. CI − SI = 5000(0.12)² = 72 ⟹ CI = **₹1,272**. Direct check: 5000 × 1.2544 − 5000 = 1272 ✓

**D5 — 8%.** 40 = 500 × r/100 ⟹ r = **8%**. P is never needed (it happens to be ₹6,250).

**D6 — (c) ₹152.50.** 20000 × 25 × 305/10⁶ = 20000 × 0.007625 = **₹152.50**. (The 2-year formula gives ₹50.)

## Set E

**E1 — 10%.** 2662/2420 = 1.1 ⟹ r = **10%**.

**E2 — ₹4,000.** 5324/4840 = 1.1 ⟹ r = 10%. P = 4840/1.21 = **₹4,000**.

**E3 — (c) ₹1,880.** Interest per year = (2600 − 2240)/3 = ₹120. P = 2240 − 3(120) = **₹1,880**. Check: 1880 + 6(120) = 2600 ✓

**E4 — 10%.** 726/660 = 1.1 ⟹ r = **10%**. A one-division question once you see the structure.

**E5 — (b) 3.** 6655/5000 = 1.331 = 1.1³ ⟹ **3 years**.

**E6 — 6.25%.** 4096 = 16³ and 4913 = 17³ ⟹ factor = 17/16 = 1.0625 ⟹ r = **6.25%**.

## Set F

**F1 — (b) 32.** 16 = 2⁴ ⟹ 4 doubling periods ⟹ 4 × 8 = **32 years**.

**F2 — 20 years.** Interest of P is earned every 4 years. Becoming 6 times needs interest 5P ⟹ **20 years**.

**F3 — (b) 24.** 81 = 3⁴ ⟹ 4 × 6 = **24 years**.

**F4 — 6.25%.** rt = 100 ⟹ r = 100/16 = **6.25%**.

**F5 — (b) 4 years.** 8 = 2³ ⟹ 12 years contains 3 doubling periods ⟹ **4 years** each.

**F6 — 40 years.** Interest 4P in 20 years ⟹ P every 5 years. 9 times needs interest 8P ⟹ **40 years**.

## Set G

**G1 — ₹1,352.** X = 2550 × 1.0816/2.04 = **₹1,352**. Check: 1352/1.04 = 1300 and 1352/1.0816 = 1250, sum 2550 ✓

**G2 — (c) ₹441.** 820 = X/1.05 + X/1.1025. Multiply by 1.1025: 904.05 = 2.05X ⟹ X = **₹441**. Check: 420 + 400 = 820 ✓ (₹420 is the present value of the first instalment only.)

**G3 — ₹3,310.** 1210 + 1100 + 1000 = **₹3,310**. (Answering ₹3,993 = 3 × 1331 ignores discounting entirely.)

**G4 — (b) ₹325.** X(1.24) + X(1.12) + X = 3.36X = 1092 ⟹ X = **₹325**.

**G5 — ₹4,200.** End of year 1: 6000 × 1.05 = 6,300; minus 2,300 leaves 4,000. End of year 2: 4000 × 1.05 = **₹4,200**.

## Set H

**H1 — ₹3,20,000.** 625000 × 0.512 = **₹3,20,000**.

**H2 — (b) 80,000.** 105800/1.3225 = **80,000**. (Subtracting 15% twice gives ≈76,441 — the planted decoy.)

**H3 — 30,000.** Net factor 0.99 ⟹ P = 29700/0.99 = **30,000**. (Assuming the rise and fall cancel gives 29,700 — wrong.)

**H4 — (c) ₹9,000.** Overall rate = 1950/15000 = 13%. Alligation (15 − 13) : (13 − 10) = 2 : 3 attaches to the 10% : 15% parts ⟹ 15% amount = (3/5)(15000) = **₹9,000**. Check: 600 + 1350 = 1950 ✓

**H5 — ₹4,000.** Overall rate = 640/8000 = 8%, exactly midway between 6% and 10% ⟹ equal split ⟹ **₹4,000** at 10%.

**H6 — (b) ₹4,00,000.** 0.9 × 0.8 = 0.72 ⟹ P = 288000/0.72 = **₹4,00,000**.

## Mixed Set

**M1 — (c) ₹1,440.** Amount after 2 years = 5000 × 1.44 = ₹7,200. Third-year interest = 7200 × 0.2 = **₹1,440**. (₹1,000 is the first-year interest.)

**M2 — ₹20,000.** P(0.15)² = 450 ⟹ 0.0225P = 450 ⟹ P = **₹20,000**.

**M3 — (b) 25.** 32 = 2⁵ ⟹ 5 × 5 = **25 years**.

**M4 — 5%.** 6615/6000 = 1.1025 = 1.05² ⟹ r = **5%**.

**M5 — (b) ₹408.** P × 0.08 × 3 = 1200 ⟹ P = ₹5,000. CI = 5000(1.0816 − 1) = **₹408**. (₹400 is the SI at 4% for 2 years.)

**M6 — 10%.** 13310/10000 = 1.331 = 1.1³ ⟹ r = **10%**.

**M7 — (b) ₹48,000.** 100000 × 0.75 × 0.8 × 0.8 = **₹48,000**. (0.75 throughout gives ₹42,187.50; 0.8 throughout gives ₹51,200.)

**M8 — ₹2,000.** Overall rate = 210/3000 = 7%. Alligation (8 − 7) : (7 − 5) = 1 : 2 attaches to the 5% : 8% parts ⟹ 8% amount = **₹2,000**. Check: 50 + 160 = 210 ✓

**M9 — (b) ₹10,816.** 10000 × 1.0816 = **₹10,816**. (₹10,800 is annual compounding — the decoy.)

**M10 — ₹605.** X = 1050 × 1.21/2.1 = **₹605**. Check: 550 + 500 = 1050 ✓

**Common error audit** — if you got a question wrong, find it here before moving on:

| Question | The error it is designed to catch |
|---|---|
| A2, B1 | reporting the amount where the interest was asked, or vice versa (§2) |
| A3 | using a period in months directly as if it were years |
| A4, F2, F6 | tripling/quintupling read as rt = 300 instead of rt = 200; SI multiples are additive |
| B2, B5, M5, M9 | applying SI machinery where CI was asked — each has an SI decoy in the options |
| B6, H6, M7 | averaging different-year rates instead of chaining the factors (§8) |
| C1, C2, C3, M9 | halving the rate but not doubling the number of periods (§3) |
| C5 | reporting the per-period rate where the annual rate was asked |
| D3, D6 | using the 2-year CI − SI formula on a 3-year question (§4) |
| D5 | hunting for P when the shortcut removes it entirely |
| E1, E2, E4 | dividing the wrong way round, or subtracting amounts instead of dividing them (§5) |
