# Means & Weighted Averages — Practice Sets

> Companion to `Means-and-Weighted-Averages.md`. Hints point to the section of the notes that contains the intended method — read the hint only after you've been stuck for a minute. Answer key with short solutions is at the bottom.
>
> **TITA** = type-in-the-answer (no options). **MCQ** = choose one.
>
> Suggested timing: 1.5 min/question for Sets A–H, and attempt the Mixed Set in one 14-minute block.

---

## Set A — Core: sums, deviations, properties

**A1.** (TITA) Find the average of 102, 107, 99, 104, 98.

*Hint: §1, deviation method — take A = 100 and average the deviations.*

**A2.** (MCQ) The average of 5 numbers is 27. Each number is increased by 4 and the result is then doubled. The new average is

- (a) 54
- (b) 58
- (c) 62
- (d) 68

*Hint: §2, the two transformation properties. Apply them in the stated order.*

**A3.** (TITA) The average of 11 consecutive integers is 17. Find the largest of them.

*Hint: §2, last bullet — for an AP the average is the middle term.*

**A4.** (TITA) The average of five distinct positive integers is 10. What is the largest possible value of any one of them?

*Hint: §1, Sum = Average × Count. To push one value up, make the other four as small as they are allowed to be — and note "distinct".*

**A5.** (MCQ) The average of 20 numbers is 45. If the average of the first 12 of them is 42, the average of the remaining 8 is

- (a) 48
- (b) 49.5
- (c) 51
- (d) 52.5

*Hint: §13, the two-line method — convert both averages to sums, subtract, divide once.*

## Set B — Weighted average and alligation

**B1.** (TITA) In a class, 30 boys average 55 marks and 20 girls average 70 marks. Find the class average.

*Hint: §3, the central formula. Then run the §3 sanity check — the answer must lie closer to 55.*

**B2.** (MCQ) A shopkeeper sells 40 kg of rice at ₹25/kg and 60 kg at ₹35/kg. The average selling price per kg is

- (a) ₹30
- (b) ₹30.50
- (c) ₹31
- (d) ₹32

*Hint: §3 — the two quantities are unequal, so a plain mean of 25 and 35 is wrong.*

**B3.** (TITA) In a class of 60 students, boys average 62 marks and girls average 74 marks, while the class averages 71. Find the number of boys.

*Hint: §4, the backward direction — use alligation to get the boys : girls ratio first.*

**B4.** (MCQ) In a company, 40% of the employees are women. Men average ₹50,000 and women average ₹60,000 per month. The company average is

- (a) ₹53,000
- (b) ₹54,000
- (c) ₹55,000
- (d) ₹56,000

*Hint: §3 — the percentages are the weights. You do not need the actual head count.*

**B5.** (TITA) Sections A, B and C have 25, 35 and 40 students averaging 70, 65 and 60 marks respectively. Find the overall average.

*Hint: §3 extended to three groups, or §13's master relation Total = Σ(size × average).*

## Set C — Replacement and addition

**C1.** (TITA) The average weight of 10 people increases by 1.5 kg when a new person replaces one weighing 58 kg. Find the weight (in kg) of the new person.

*Hint: §5, the general rule — new value = old value + (n × change in average).*

**C2.** (MCQ) The average of 12 numbers is 25. When one number is removed, the average of the rest is 24. The removed number is

- (a) 13
- (b) 25
- (c) 34
- (d) 36

*Hint: §5 — work with totals: 12 × 25 minus 11 × 24.*

**C3.** (TITA) The average age of 15 students is 14 years. When the teacher's age is included, the average becomes 15 years. Find the teacher's age.

*Hint: §5, the adding variant — new member's value = (n+1)A′ − nA. It is not 15.*

**C4.** (MCQ) The average score of 11 players is 32. The captain, who scored 50, leaves and a new player joins; the average becomes 31. The new player's score is

- (a) 28
- (b) 39
- (c) 41
- (d) 43

*Hint: §5 — total before, total after, and the 50 that left. Three sums, one subtraction.*

**C5.** (TITA) In a group of 8 people, two members weighing 55 kg and 60 kg are replaced by two new members, and the average weight rises by 3 kg. Find the combined weight (in kg) of the two new members.

*Hint: §5 — the total change is still n × (change in average), regardless of how many members were swapped.*

## Set D — Correction problems

**D1.** (TITA) The average of 25 numbers was computed as 40, but one number was read as 36 instead of 63. Find the correct average.

*Hint: §6 — adjust the sum by the error first, then divide once.*

**D2.** (TITA) The average of 10 numbers is 62. It is later found that 85 was read as 58, and 45 was read as 54. Find the correct average.

*Hint: §6 — the two errors go in opposite directions. Compute the net correction to the sum.*

**D3.** (MCQ) The average marks of 50 students was found to be 72. It was then discovered that two students' marks were entered as 48 and 55 instead of the correct 84 and 45. The correct average is

- (a) 71.48
- (b) 72.48
- (c) 72.52
- (d) 73.52

*Hint: §6 — net error = (84 − 48) + (45 − 55). Watch the sign of the second one.*

## Set E — Average speed

**E1.** (TITA) A car travels from A to B at 30 km/h and returns at 70 km/h. Find the average speed (in km/h) for the whole journey.

*Hint: §7, equal distances ⟹ harmonic mean 2ab/(a+b). It is not 50.*

**E2.** (MCQ) A man cycles at 12 km/h for 3 hours and then at 18 km/h for 3 hours. His average speed is

- (a) 14 km/h
- (b) 14.4 km/h
- (c) 15 km/h
- (d) 16 km/h

*Hint: §7 — read for whether time or distance is equal. Here it is time.*

**E3.** (TITA) A man covers three equal stretches of a road at 20 km/h, 30 km/h and 60 km/h. Find his average speed (in km/h).

*Hint: §7, last line — average = 3/(1/a + 1/b + 1/c).*

**E4.** (TITA) A person travels 60 km at 30 km/h and the next 90 km at 45 km/h. Find the average speed (in km/h).

*Hint: §7, the definition — total distance / total time. The distances are unequal, so no shortcut formula applies.*

## Set F — AM, GM, HM and maxima–minima

**F1.** (MCQ) The AM of two positive numbers is 10 and their GM is 8. Their HM is

- (a) 5
- (b) 6
- (c) 6.4
- (d) 12.5

*Hint: §8 — GM² = AM × HM.*

**F2.** (TITA) Two positive numbers have a sum of 30. Find their maximum possible product.

*Hint: §8, general principle — for a fixed sum the product is maximised when the terms are equal.*

**F3.** (TITA) The AM of two positive numbers is 13 and their GM is 12. Find the positive difference between the two numbers.

*Hint: §8 with §1 of `Algebra.md` — (a − b)² = (a + b)² − 4ab.*

**F4.** (MCQ) A quantity grows by 25% in the first year and by 80% in the second. The average annual growth rate is

- (a) 45%
- (b) 50%
- (c) 52.5%
- (d) 55%

*Hint: §9 — the quantities multiply, so take the geometric mean of the growth factors, not the arithmetic mean of the rates.*

**F5.** (TITA) For positive reals a and b, find the minimum value of (a + b)(1/a + 1/b).

*Hint: §8 — expand first; you will be left with 2 + (a/b + b/a), and x + 1/x ≥ 2.*

## Set G — AP-type averages, median and mode

**G1.** (TITA) Find the average of the first 75 natural numbers.

*Hint: §10, first bullet — (n+1)/2.*

**G2.** (MCQ) The average of the first 20 odd natural numbers is

- (a) 19
- (b) 20
- (c) 21
- (d) 40

*Hint: §10 — the sum of the first n odd numbers is n².*

**G3.** (TITA) The average of the first n even natural numbers is 31. Find n.

*Hint: §10 — the average of the first n even numbers is n + 1.*

**G4.** (MCQ) For the data set 4, 7, 7, 9, 13, 100, the median is

- (a) 7
- (b) 8
- (c) 9
- (d) 23.33

*Hint: §11 — with an even count the median is the average of the two middle terms. One option is the mean, which the outlier has dragged upward.*

## Set H — Unknown counts and sub-group reconstruction

**H1.** (TITA) A class of 50 students averages 60 marks. A sub-group of 20 averages 45. Find the average of the remaining 30.

*Hint: §13, third worked example — totals, subtract, divide once.*

**H2.** (TITA) The average age of a group is 25 years. When 6 people of average age 32 join, the group's average becomes 27. Find the original number of people.

*Hint: §12, the alligation shortcut — get original : new as a ratio, then scale using the 6.*

**H3.** (MCQ) A class averages 72 marks. The 15 girls average 80 and the boys average 66. The number of boys is

- (a) 18
- (b) 20
- (c) 24
- (d) 25

*Hint: §13, missing sub-group size — alligation gives boys : girls directly.*

**H4.** (TITA) Of five numbers, the average of the first three is 14 and the average of the last three is 18. If the third number is 16, find the average of all five.

*Hint: §13, the nested-group version — the two stated groups overlap in the third number, so subtract it once.*

**H5.** (TITA) A firm has three departments. Department A has 12 people averaging ₹30,000; department B has 18 people averaging ₹45,000. All 40 employees average ₹42,000. Find department C's average salary (in ₹).

*Hint: §13, master relation — Total = Σ(size × average). First work out how many people are in C.*

**H6.** (MCQ) The average of 7 numbers is 40. The average of the first four is 35 and the average of the last four is 47. The fourth number is

- (a) 42
- (b) 44
- (c) 48
- (d) 52

*Hint: §13 — the two groups of four overlap in exactly one number.*

# Answer Key

## Set A

**A1 — 102.** Take A = 100. Deviations: +2, +7, −1, +4, −2, summing to 10. Average = 100 + 10/5 = 102.

**A2 — (c) 62.** Adding 4 raises the average to 31; doubling then doubles it to 62.

**A3 — 22.** For 11 consecutive integers the average is the 6th (middle) term, so the middle is 17. The numbers run 12 to 22; largest = 22.

**A4 — 40.** Total = 5 × 10 = 50. To maximise one value, minimise the other four distinct positive integers: 1 + 2 + 3 + 4 = 10. Largest = 50 − 10 = 40.

**A5 — (b) 49.5.** Total = 900. First twelve sum to 504, so the rest sum to 396. Average = 396/8 = 49.5.

## Set B

**B1 — 61.** (30 × 55 + 20 × 70)/50 = (1650 + 1400)/50 = 61. Check: 61 is nearer 55 than 70, and boys are the larger group ✓.

**B2 — (c) ₹31.** (40 × 25 + 60 × 35)/100 = (1000 + 2100)/100 = 31. (Nearer 35, because more rice was sold at 35 ✓.)

**B3 — 15.** Alligation about 71: boys : girls = (74 − 71) : (71 − 62) = 3 : 9 = 1 : 3. Of 60 students, boys = 60 × 1/4 = 15.

**B4 — (b) ₹54,000.** Weights 60 : 40. (60 × 50 + 40 × 60)/100 = (3000 + 2400)/100 = 54 thousand.

**B5 — 64.25.** (25 × 70 + 35 × 65 + 40 × 60)/100 = (1750 + 2275 + 2400)/100 = 6425/100 = 64.25.

## Set C

**C1 — 73 kg.** Total increase = 10 × 1.5 = 15 kg. New person = 58 + 15 = 73.

**C2 — (d) 36.** 12 × 25 − 11 × 24 = 300 − 264 = 36.

**C3 — 30 years.** 16 × 15 − 15 × 14 = 240 − 210 = 30. (Not 15 — the teacher must also pull the other fifteen up.)

**C4 — (b) 39.** Original total = 352. After the swap the total is 11 × 31 = 341. Without the captain the other ten total 352 − 50 = 302, so the new player scored 341 − 302 = 39.

**C5 — 139 kg.** Total increase = 8 × 3 = 24 kg. Combined new weight = (55 + 60) + 24 = 139.

## Set D

**D1 — 41.08.** The sum was understated by 63 − 36 = 27. Correct average = 40 + 27/25 = 40 + 1.08 = 41.08.

**D2 — 63.8.** Errors: 85 − 58 = +27 and 45 − 54 = −9, net +18. Correct average = 62 + 18/10 = 63.8.

**D3 — (c) 72.52.** Net error = (84 − 48) + (45 − 55) = 36 − 10 = +26. Correct average = 72 + 26/50 = 72.52.

## Set E

**E1 — 42 km/h.** Equal distances ⟹ 2(30)(70)/(30 + 70) = 4200/100 = 42.

**E2 — (c) 15 km/h.** Equal times ⟹ plain average (12 + 18)/2 = 15.

**E3 — 30 km/h.** 3/(1/20 + 1/30 + 1/60) = 3/(6/60) = 3/0.1 = 30.

**E4 — 37.5 km/h.** Times: 60/30 = 2 h and 90/45 = 2 h, so 4 h for 150 km ⟹ 37.5 km/h.

## Set F

**F1 — (c) 6.4.** GM² = AM × HM ⟹ 64 = 10 × HM ⟹ HM = 6.4.

**F2 — 225.** Product is maximal at equality: 15 × 15 = 225.

**F3 — 10.** a + b = 26 and ab = 144. (a − b)² = 676 − 576 = 100 ⟹ difference = 10. (The numbers are 18 and 8.)

**F4 — (b) 50%.** Factors 1.25 and 1.80 multiply to 2.25; √2.25 = 1.5 ⟹ 50% per year. (The arithmetic mean, 52.5%, is the trap.)

**F5 — 4.** Expand: (a + b)(1/a + 1/b) = 2 + a/b + b/a ≥ 2 + 2 = 4, with equality at a = b.

## Set G

**G1 — 38.** (n + 1)/2 = 76/2 = 38.

**G2 — (b) 20.** Sum of the first 20 odd numbers = 20² = 400; average = 400/20 = 20.

**G3 — 30.** Average of the first n even numbers = n + 1 = 31 ⟹ n = 30.

**G4 — (b) 8.** Sorted, the two middle terms are 7 and 9 ⟹ median = 8. The mean, 140/6 ≈ 23.33, is dragged up by the outlier 100.

## Set H

**H1 — 70.** Total = 3000; sub-group total = 900 ⟹ remainder = 2100 over 30 = 70.

**H2 — 15.** Alligation about 27: original : new = (32 − 27) : (27 − 25) = 5 : 2. New = 6 ⟹ original = 15. (Check: (375 + 192)/21 = 27 ✓.)

**H3 — (b) 20.** Alligation about 72: boys : girls = (80 − 72) : (72 − 66) = 8 : 6 = 4 : 3. Girls = 15 ⟹ boys = 20.

**H4 — 16.** First three sum to 42, last three to 54; total 96 counts the third number twice. Sum of all five = 96 − 16 = 80 ⟹ average = 16.

**H5 — ₹51,000.** Total = 40 × 42 = 1680 (thousands). A + B = 360 + 810 = 1170. C has 40 − 30 = 10 people totalling 510 ⟹ average ₹51,000.

**H6 — (c) 48.** First four sum to 140, last four to 188; total 328 counts the fourth number twice. All seven sum to 280 ⟹ fourth = 328 − 280 = 48.

## Mixed Set

**M1 — (a) 20.** Alligation about 55: boys : girls = (65 − 55) : (55 − 50) = 2 : 1 ⟹ girls = 60 × 1/3 = 20.

**M2 — 48 km/h.** Equal distances ⟹ 2(40)(60)/100 = 48.

**M3 — (d) 42.** Adding 3 gives 21; doubling gives 42.

**M4 — 66.6 kg.** Total = 408; after the person leaves, 333 over 5 = 66.6.

**M5 — (c) 12.** GM = √(AM × HM) = √(15 × 9.6) = √144 = 12.

**M6 — 56.** First six sum to 294, last six to 312; total 606 counts the sixth twice. All eleven sum to 550 ⟹ sixth = 606 − 550 = 56.

**M7 — (c) 24.9.** Net error = (51 − 15) + (23 − 32) = 36 − 9 = +27. Correct average = 24 + 27/30 = 24.9.

**M8 — 12.5 years.** Current total = 100, so the other four members total 90 now. Ten years ago they totalled 90 − 40 = 50, giving an average of 12.5 for the family that existed then.
