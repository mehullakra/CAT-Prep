# Ratio, Proportion & Variation — Practice Sets

> Companion to `03-Ratio-Proportion-Variation.md`. Hints point to the section of the notes that contains the intended method — read the hint only after you've been stuck for a minute. Answer key with short solutions is at the bottom.
>
> **TITA** = type-in-the-answer (no options). **MCQ** = choose one.
>
> Suggested timing: 1.5 min/question for Sets A–G, and attempt the Mixed Set in one timed block.
>
> **Write k the moment a ratio appears.** Almost every question below is "find k, then answer what was asked". If you find yourself carrying two unknowns through a ratio question, you've skipped §1.

---

## Set A — Forming & combining ratios

**A1.** (MCQ) The ratio of ₹3 to 75 paise is

- (a) 1 : 25
- (b) 3 : 75
- (c) 4 : 1
- (d) 1 : 4

*Hint: §1 corollary and the first row of the Traps table — convert to a common unit before forming the ratio.*

**A2.** (TITA) If A : B = 3 : 4 and B : C = 6 : 7, express A : B : C in lowest terms and give the sum of the three terms.

*Hint: §3 — equalise B with the LCM of 4 and 6, then reduce.*

**A3.** (MCQ) If 3 is added to both terms of the ratio 5 : 7, the new ratio is

- (a) 4 : 5
- (b) 8 : 7
- (c) 2 : 3
- (d) 5 : 10

*Hint: §2 — before computing, note the answer must be closer to 1 : 1 than 5 : 7 is. That alone kills two options.*

**A4.** (TITA) The sub-duplicate ratio of x : 49 is 3 : 7. Find x.

*Hint: §2, duplicate ratios — sub-duplicate means √x : √49.*

**A5.** (MCQ) If A : B = 2 : 3, B : C = 4 : 5 and C : D = 6 : 7, then A : D is

- (a) 16 : 35
- (b) 8 : 35
- (c) 4 : 7
- (d) 35 : 16

*Hint: §3 — extend the chain one link at a time. Do not try to combine all three ratios in a single step.*

## Set B — Proportion, componendo & dividendo

**B1.** (TITA) Find the fourth proportional to 4, 6 and 14.

*Hint: §4 — 4 : 6 = 14 : x. Note the position of the unknown before cross-multiplying.*

**B2.** (TITA) Find the third proportional to 9 and 12.

*Hint: §4 — third proportional to a, b is b²/a.*

**B3.** (MCQ) The mean proportional between 8 and 32 is

- (a) 16
- (b) 20
- (c) 24
- (d) 40

*Hint: §4 — mean proportional is the geometric mean, not the arithmetic one.*

**B4.** (TITA) If (4x + 3y)/(4x − 3y) = 5/3, find x/y.

*Hint: §5, reverse componendo–dividendo — set a = 4x, b = 3y first, then unwind at the end.*

**B5.** (MCQ) If a : b = c : d = 3 : 4, then (2a + 3c)/(2b + 3d) equals

- (a) 3/4
- (b) 5/7
- (c) 6/7
- (d) cannot be determined

*Hint: §9, the weighted form of the k-theorem — any weights give back the same ratio.*

## Set C — Ratio changes after adding/removing

**C1.** (TITA) The ratio of boys to girls in a class is 7 : 5. If 8 more girls join, the ratio becomes 7 : 6. Find the number of boys.

*Hint: §6 — the boys don't change, so their term keeps the same k on both sides.*

**C2.** (MCQ) The present ages of A and B are in the ratio 7 : 5. After 6 years the ratio will be 4 : 3. A's present age is

- (a) 30 years
- (b) 36 years
- (c) 42 years
- (d) 48 years

*Hint: §6 — add 6 to both terms. Sanity check: 4 : 3 is closer to 1 : 1 than 7 : 5, as it must be.*

**C3.** (TITA) Two numbers are in the ratio 3 : 4. If each is increased by 8, the ratio becomes 4 : 5. Find the larger number.

*Hint: §6 — one variable k, one equation, cross-multiply.*

**C4.** (MCQ) Two numbers are in the ratio 5 : 9. If 3 is subtracted from each, the ratio becomes 1 : 2. The sum of the numbers is

- (a) 28
- (b) 42
- (c) 56
- (d) 70

*Hint: §2 and §6 — subtracting moves the ratio away from 1 : 1, and 1 : 2 is indeed further out than 5 : 9.*

**C5.** (TITA) The ratio of A's to B's present age is 4 : 3. Eight years ago it was 3 : 2. Find B's present age.

*Hint: §6 — subtract 8 from both terms. Going backwards moves the ratio away from 1 : 1.*

## Set D — Dividing a sum & partnership

**D1.** (TITA) ₹1,800 is divided among A, B and C such that A : B = 2 : 3 and B : C = 3 : 4. Find C's share (in ₹).

*Hint: §3 then §7 — B is already the same number in both ratios, so no LCM step is needed.*

**D2.** (MCQ) ₹1,860 is divided among three people in the inverse ratio 2 : 3 : 5. The smallest share is

- (a) ₹300
- (b) ₹360
- (c) ₹400
- (d) ₹600

*Hint: §7, inverse ratio division, and the last row of the Traps table — take reciprocals and clear them with the LCM first.*

**D3.** (TITA) A invests ₹15,000 for 8 months and B invests ₹18,000 for 10 months. A profit of ₹12,000 is divided between them. Find A's share (in ₹).

*Hint: §10 — profit ∝ capital × time. Reduce the products before dividing.*

**D4.** (MCQ) A, B and C invest capitals in the ratio 3 : 4 : 5 for periods in the ratio 4 : 3 : 2. Out of a profit of ₹17,000, C receives

- (a) ₹4,000
- (b) ₹5,000
- (c) ₹6,000
- (d) ₹6,800

*Hint: §10 — multiply the two ratios term by term, then reduce before splitting.*

**D5.** (TITA) A sum of ₹3,600 is divided among A, B and C so that A gets half of what B gets and B gets one-third of what C gets. Find C's share (in ₹).

*Hint: §7 — write everything in terms of B, then clear the fractions to get a whole-number ratio.*

**D6.** (MCQ) A invests ₹20,000 and B invests ₹30,000. A is the working partner and receives 10% of the profit as salary; the remainder is divided in the ratio of capital. Out of a total profit of ₹22,000, A receives in all

- (a) ₹8,800
- (b) ₹10,120
- (c) ₹11,000
- (d) ₹9,900

*Hint: §10, working vs sleeping partner — the salary comes off the top before anything is split.*

## Set E — Variation

**E1.** (TITA) x varies directly as y². When y = 3, x = 45. Find x when y = 5.

*Hint: §8 — write x = ky², find k from the first data point, then substitute.*

**E2.** (MCQ) a varies inversely as b. When b = 5, a = 12. When b = 15, a equals

- (a) 4
- (b) 6
- (c) 20
- (d) 36

*Hint: §8 — inverse variation means ab = k, a constant product. Sanity check: b tripled, so a should shrink.*

**E3.** (TITA) The cost of a gem varies as the cube of its weight. A gem weighing 8 g costs ₹6,400. It breaks into two pieces weighing 3 g and 5 g. Find the loss (in ₹).

*Hint: §8, worked example — but the power is 3 here, so the w² shortcut does not apply. Find k explicitly.*

**E4.** (MCQ) The value of a diamond varies as the square of its weight. A diamond worth ₹16,000 breaks into two pieces whose weights are in the ratio 1 : 3. The loss is

- (a) ₹4,000
- (b) ₹6,000
- (c) ₹8,000
- (d) ₹10,000

*Hint: §8, the generalisation — loss fraction = 2ab/(a + b)². No need to know the actual weight.*

**E5.** (TITA) x is partly constant and partly varies as y. When y = 10, x = 250; when y = 20, x = 400. Find x when y = 30.

*Hint: §8, partial variation — x = m + ny, and two data points give two equations.*

**E6.** (MCQ) If a ∝ b and b ∝ 1/c, then

- (a) a ∝ c
- (b) a ∝ 1/c
- (c) a ∝ c²
- (d) no relation exists between a and c

*Hint: §8 — chain the constants: a = k₁b and b = k₂/c.*

## Set F — Equal ratios & the k-theorem

**F1.** (TITA) If a/3 = b/4 = c/7, find (a + b + c)/c.

*Hint: §9 — set the common value to k and write a, b, c in terms of k.*

**F2.** (MCQ) If x : y : z = 2 : 3 : 5, then (x² + y² + z²)/(xy + yz + zx) equals

- (a) 31/38
- (b) 19/16
- (c) 38/31
- (d) 2

*Hint: §9 — numerator and denominator are both homogeneous of degree 2, so every k² cancels.*

**F3.** (TITA) If a : b = 3 : 5 and b : c = 2 : 3, find (a + c)/b.

*Hint: §3 to build a : b : c, then §9 to evaluate.*

**F4.** (MCQ) If a/2 = b/3 = c/4, then (3a + 2b + c)/(a + b + c) equals

- (a) 9/16
- (b) 4/3
- (c) 16/9
- (d) 2

*Hint: §9 — substitute a = 2k, b = 3k, c = 4k and cancel k.*

**F5.** (TITA) Three numbers are in the ratio 3 : 4 : 5 and the sum of their squares is 1250. Find the largest number.

*Hint: §1 — write them as 3k, 4k, 5k. The equation becomes a single one in k².*

## Set G — Redistribution, exchange & savings

**G1.** (TITA) A and B have money in the ratio 4 : 3. If A gives ₹50 to B, the ratio becomes 6 : 5. Find A's original amount (in ₹).

*Hint: §12 — what A loses, B gains. Check afterwards that the total is unchanged.*

**G2.** (MCQ) The salaries of A and B are in the ratio 5 : 4. A's salary rises by 15% and B's rises by ₹1,000, after which the ratio is 23 : 20. B's new salary is

- (a) ₹4,600
- (b) ₹5,000
- (c) ₹5,750
- (d) ₹4,000

*Hint: §12, second worked example — this is an increase, not a transfer, so the total is not conserved. Answer the question actually asked: the **new** salary.*

**G3.** (TITA) A, B and C have amounts in the ratio 3 : 4 : 5. A gives ₹50 to B, and B gives ₹100 to C. The final ratio becomes 2 : 3 : 7. Find the total amount (in ₹).

*Hint: §12, three-way transfer — B both receives and gives, so write both movements. Use the third comparison as a free check.*

**G4.** (MCQ) The savings of A and B are in the ratio 7 : 5. A spends ₹1,000 and B spends ₹800, after which the ratio becomes 2 : 1. B's original savings were

- (a) ₹800
- (b) ₹1,000
- (c) ₹1,200
- (d) ₹1,400

*Hint: §12 — different amounts are removed, so this is not conservation; set up one equation in k.*

**G5.** (TITA) The incomes of A and B are in the ratio 5 : 4 and their expenditures in the ratio 3 : 2. If each saves ₹6,000, find A's income (in ₹).

*Hint: §12 — two independent ratios means two variables, x and y, not one k. Income − expenditure = savings gives two equations.*

**G6.** (MCQ) A bag contains ₹1, 50-paise and 25-paise coins in the ratio 5 : 6 : 8, totalling ₹210. The number of 25-paise coins is

- (a) 105
- (b) 126
- (c) 168
- (d) 210

*Hint: §1 and the Traps table — the ratio is of the **number** of coins, so convert each count into value before summing.*

# Answer key

## Set A

**A1 — (c) 4 : 1.** Convert to paise: 300 : 75 = **4 : 1**. Option (b) is the raw unconverted ratio and (a) is its inverse — both are the trap.

**A2 — 35.** LCM of 4 and 6 is 12: A : B = 9 : 12 and B : C = 12 : 14, so A : B : C = 9 : 12 : 14. Sum = 9 + 12 + 14 = ****35****.

**A3 — (a) 4 : 5.** (5 + 3) : (7 + 3) = 8 : 10 = **4 : 5**. Check the §2 rule: 5/7 ≈ 0.71 and 4/5 = 0.80, which is closer to 1 ✓.

**A4 — 9.** √x : 7 = 3 : 7 ⟹ √x = 3 ⟹ x = ****9****.

**A5 — (a) 16 : 35.** A : B : C = 8 : 12 : 15 (LCM on B). Now equalise C with D using LCM(15, 6) = 30: A : B : C = 16 : 24 : 30 and C : D = 30 : 35, so A : B : C : D = 16 : 24 : 30 : 35 ⟹ A : D = ****16 : 35****.

## Set B

**B1 — 21.** 4 : 6 = 14 : x ⟹ x = 6 × 14/4 = ****21****.

**B2 — 16.** 9 : 12 = 12 : x ⟹ x = 144/9 = ****16****.

**B3 — (a) 16.** √(8 × 32) = √256 = ****16****. (20 is the arithmetic mean — that's the trap.)

**B4 — 3.** Let a = 4x, b = 3y. (a + b)/(a − b) = 5/3 ⟹ a/b = (5 + 3)/(5 − 3) = 4. So 4x/3y = 4 ⟹ x/y = ****3****.

**B5 — (a) 3/4.** With a/b = c/d = k = 3/4, any weighted combination (2a + 3c)/(2b + 3d) = k = ****3/4****. Option (d) is the trap — the individual values are unknown but the ratio is not.

## Set C

**C1 — 56.** Boys = 7k, girls = 5k. 7k/(5k + 8) = 7/6 ⟹ 42k = 35k + 56 ⟹ k = 8 ⟹ boys = **56**. (Girls: 40 → 48; **56** : 48 = 7 : 6 ✓)

**C2 — (c) 42 years.** (7k + 6)/(5k + 6) = 4/3 ⟹ 21k + 18 = 20k + 24 ⟹ k = 6 ⟹ A = **42**, B = 30. Check: 48 : 36 = 4 : 3 ✓.

**C3 — 32.** (3k + 8)/(4k + 8) = 4/5 ⟹ 15k + 40 = 16k + 32 ⟹ k = 8 ⟹ numbers 24 and **32**. Check: **32** : 40 = 4 : 5 ✓.

**C4 — (b) 42.** (5k − 3)/(9k − 3) = 1/2 ⟹ 10k − 6 = 9k − 3 ⟹ k = 3 ⟹ numbers 15 and 27, sum = ****42****.

**C5 — 24.** (4k − 8)/(3k − 8) = 3/2 ⟹ 8k − 16 = 9k − 24 ⟹ k = 8 ⟹ A = 32, B = **24**. Check: **24** : 16 = 3 : 2 ✓.

## Set D

**D1 — 800.** B is 3 in both ratios already, so A : B : C = 2 : 3 : 4, sum 9. Each part = 1800/9 = 200 ⟹ C = ****₹800****.

**D2 — (b) ₹360.** Inverse ratio: 1/2 : 1/3 : 1/5 = 15 : 10 : 6 (×30), sum 31. Each part = 1860/31 = 60 ⟹ shares ₹900, ₹600, ****₹360****. The smallest share goes to the largest original term, as it must.

**D3 — 4800.** Ratio = 15000 × 8 : 18000 × 10 = 120000 : 180000 = 2 : 3, sum 5. A = 12000 × 2/5 = ****₹4,800****.

**D4 — (b) ₹5,000.** Profit ratio = 3×4 : 4×3 : 5×2 = 12 : 12 : 10 = 6 : 6 : 5, sum 17. C = 17000 × 5/17 = ****₹5,000****.

**D5 — 2400.** A = B/2 and C = 3B ⟹ A : B : C = 1/2 : 1 : 3 = 1 : 2 : 6, sum 9. Each part = 400 ⟹ C = ****₹2,400****.

**D6 — (b) ₹10,120.** Salary = 10% of 22000 = ₹2,200. Remainder ₹19,800 splits 2 : 3 ⟹ A's capital share = 19800 × 2/5 = ₹7,920. Total = 2200 + 7920 = ****₹10,120****. (₹8,800 is the trap — splitting the whole profit 2 : 5 of 22000, ignoring the salary.)

## Set E

**E1 — 125.** x = ky² ⟹ 45 = 9k ⟹ k = 5. At y = 5: x = 5 × 25 = ****125****.

**E2 — (a) 4.** ab = k = 12 × 5 = 60 ⟹ a = 60/15 = ****4****. Sanity check: b tripled, so a fell to a third ✓.

**E3 — 4500.** C = kw³ ⟹ 6400 = k(512) ⟹ k = 12.5. Pieces: 12.5(27) + 12.5(125) = 337.5 + 1562.5 = 1900. Loss = 6400 − 1900 = ****₹4,500****.

**E4 — (b) ₹6,000.** Loss fraction = 2ab/(a + b)² = 2(1)(3)/16 = 3/8 ⟹ 16000 × 3/8 = ****₹6,000****.

**E5 — 550.** x = m + ny. m + 10n = 250 and m + 20n = 400 ⟹ 10n = 150 ⟹ n = 15, m = 100. At y = 30: x = 100 + 450 = ****550****.

**E6 — (b) a ∝ 1/c.** a = k₁b and b = k₂/c ⟹ a = k₁k₂/c, so ****a ∝ 1/c****.

## Set F

**F1 — 2.** a = 3k, b = 4k, c = 7k ⟹ (14k)/(7k) = ****2****.

**F2 — (c) 38/31.** With x = 2k, y = 3k, z = 5k: numerator = 38k², denominator = 6k² + 15k² + 10k² = 31k² ⟹ ****38/31****.

**F3 — 21/10.** LCM on b: a : b = 6 : 10 and b : c = 10 : 15 ⟹ a : b : c = 6 : 10 : 15. (a + c)/b = 21/10 = ****2.1****.

**F4 — (c) 16/9.** a = 2k, b = 3k, c = 4k ⟹ (6k + 6k + 4k)/(9k) = ****16/9****.

**F5 — 25.** 9k² + 16k² + 25k² = 50k² = 1250 ⟹ k² = 25 ⟹ k = 5 ⟹ numbers 15, 20, ****25****.

## Set G

**G1 — 1100.** (4k − 50)/(3k + 50) = 6/5 ⟹ 20k − 250 = 18k + 300 ⟹ k = 275 ⟹ A = ****₹1,100****, B = ₹825. Conservation check: 1100 + 825 = 1925 and 1050 + 875 = 1925 ✓, and 1050 : 875 = 6 : 5 ✓.

**G2 — (b) ₹5,000.** 5.75k/(4k + 1000) = 23/20 ⟹ 115k = 92k + 23000 ⟹ k = 1000. Original: A = ₹5,000, B = ₹4,000. New: A = ₹5,750, B = ****₹5,000****. (₹4,000 is B's *old* salary — the trap.) No conservation check here: an increase changes the total.

**G3 — 600.** After: A = 3k − 50, B = 4k + 50 − 100 = 4k − 50, C = 5k + 100. From A : B = 2 : 3: 3(3k − 50) = 2(4k − 50) ⟹ 9k − 150 = 8k − 100 ⟹ k = 50. Amounts 100, 150, 350 ⟹ total = ****₹600**** = 12k ✓. Free check with the unused pair: B : C = 150 : 350 = 3 : 7 ✓.

**G4 — (b) ₹1,000.** (7k − 1000)/(5k − 800) = 2/1 ⟹ 7k − 1000 = 10k − 1600 ⟹ 3k = 600 ⟹ k = 200. A = ₹1,400, B = ****₹1,000****. Check: 400 : 200 = 2 : 1 ✓.

**G5 — 15000.** Incomes 5x, 4x; expenditures 3y, 2y. 5x − 3y = 6000 and 4x − 2y = 6000 ⟹ from the second, y = 2x − 3000. Substituting: 5x − 6x + 9000 = 6000 ⟹ x = 3000 ⟹ A's income = ****₹15,000****. Check: incomes 15000 and 12000, expenditures 9000 and 6000, savings 6000 each ✓.

**G6 — (c) 168.** Counts 5k, 6k, 8k ⟹ value = 5k + 3k + 2k = 10k rupees = 210 ⟹ k = 21. 25-paise coins = 8 × 21 = ****168****. (Treating 5 : 6 : 8 as a ratio of values is the trap.)

## Mixed Set

**M1 — (c) ₹5,000.** LCM on B: A : B = 3 : 4 and B : C = 4 : 10 ⟹ A : B : C = 3 : 4 : 10, sum 17. Each part = 8500/17 = 500 ⟹ C = ****₹5,000****.

**M2 — 6/5.** (a − b)/(a + b) = 1/3 ⟹ (a + b)/(a − b) = 3 ⟹ a/b = (3 + 1)/(3 − 1) = 2. With a = 5x, b = 3y: 5x/3y = 2 ⟹ x/y = 6/5 = ****1.2****.

**M3 — (a) 15.** (5k + 9)/(8k + 9) = 8/11 ⟹ 55k + 99 = 64k + 72 ⟹ 9k = 27 ⟹ k = 3 ⟹ numbers ****15**** and 24. Check: 24 : 33 = 8 : 11 ✓.

**M4 — 2400.** Loss fraction = 2(3)(2)/(5)² = 12/25 ⟹ 5000 × 12/25 = ****₹2,400****.

**M5 — (b) ₹5,000.** Ratio = 8×6 : 10×8 : 12×10 = 48 : 80 : 120 = 6 : 10 : 15, sum 31. Each part = 15500/31 = 500 ⟹ B = ****₹5,000****.

**M6 — 36.** k² + 4k² + 9k² = 14k² = 504 ⟹ k² = 36 ⟹ k = 6 ⟹ numbers 6, 12, 18, sum = ****36****.

**M7 — (b) ₹400.** Total = 5k, so each ends with 2.5k. 3k − 100 = 2.5k ⟹ k = 200 ⟹ A = ₹600, B = ****₹400****. Check: 500 and 500 ✓.

**M8 — 2.** xy² = k = 8 × 9 = 72 ⟹ x = 72/36 = ****2****.

**M9 — (c) ₹800.** Inverse ratio: 1/3 : 1/4 : 1/6 = 4 : 3 : 2 (×12), sum 9. Each part = 200 ⟹ shares ****₹800****, ₹600, ₹400.

**M10 — 18000.** 9x − 4y = 2000 and 7x − 3y = 2000. Multiply the first by 3 and the second by 4: 27x − 12y = 6000 and 28x − 12y = 8000 ⟹ x = 2000 ⟹ first income = ****₹18,000****. Check: incomes 18000 and 14000, expenditures 16000 and 12000, savings 2000 each ✓.

**Common error audit** — if you got a question wrong, find it here before moving on:

| Question | The error it is designed to catch |
|---|---|
| A1, G6 | forming a ratio before converting to a common unit, or ratio-of-counts vs ratio-of-values |
| A2, A5, F3, M1 | multiplying chained ratios directly instead of equalising the common term |
| A3, C4 | not using the "towards / away from 1 : 1" check to eliminate options |
| B3 | using the arithmetic mean where the mean proportional (geometric mean) is asked |
| B4, M2 | applying componendo–dividendo without first identifying a and b correctly |
| B5 | assuming the individual values are needed when only the ratio is |
| C1, C2, C3, C5 | letting the unchanged quantity drift to a second variable instead of keeping one k |
| D2, M9 | splitting in the stated ratio rather than the ratio of reciprocals |
| D6 | splitting the whole profit by capital and ignoring the working partner's salary |
| E2, E6 | treating inverse variation as direct |
| E3 | using the w² loss shortcut where the power is 3 |
| E5 | forcing pure variation on a "partly constant" statement |
| F2, F4 | not cancelling k — usually a sign the k-substitution was skipped |
| G1, G3 | mishandling a two-way movement, or forgetting a transfer conserves the total |
| G2 | reporting the original value where the new one was asked |
| G5, M10 | using one k when the question gives two independent ratios |
