# Time, Speed & Distance — Practice Sets

> Companion to `08-Time-Speed-Distance.md`. Hints point to the section of the notes that contains the intended method — read the hint only after you've been stuck for a minute. Answer key with short solutions is at the bottom.
>
> **TITA** = type-in-the-answer (no options). **MCQ** = choose one.
>
> Suggested timing: 1.5 min/question for Sets A–H, and attempt the Mixed Set in one timed block.
>
> **Before computing, name the quantity being held constant.** Distance constant ⟹ invert the speed ratio. Time constant ⟹ distances are proportional to speeds. Naming it first kills the average-speed and ratio-inversion errors in one move.
>
> Convert units before anything else: km/h → m/s is ×5/18. Mixing units mid-solution is silent — the arithmetic looks fine and the answer is off by 3.6.

## Set A — The inverse-ratio core

**A1.** (TITA) Walking at 5/6 of his usual speed, a man reaches office 8 minutes late. Find his usual time (in minutes).

*Hint: §1 — speed ratio 5 : 6 inverts to a time ratio of 6 : 5. The 8 minutes is one part.*

**A2.** (MCQ) Increasing his speed by 20%, a man reaches 12 minutes early. His usual time is

- (a) 60 min
- (b) 66 min
- (c) 72 min
- (d) 84 min

*Hint: §1 — a 20% increase is a speed ratio of 6 : 5 (new : usual), so the time ratio is 5 : 6.*

**A3.** (TITA) A boy walking at 4 km/h reaches school 5 minutes late; walking at 5 km/h he is 10 minutes early. Find the distance to school (in km).

*Hint: §1 — the two journeys differ by 15 minutes in total. Set up d/4 − d/5 = 15/60.*

**A4.** (MCQ) A train travelling at 3/5 of its usual speed is 40 minutes late. Its usual time for the journey is

- (a) 40 min
- (b) 60 min
- (c) 80 min
- (d) 100 min

*Hint: §1 — here the difference is 2 parts, not 1. Divide before multiplying.*

**A5.** (TITA) If a man reduces his speed by 25%, he takes 20 minutes more. Find his usual time (in minutes).

*Hint: §1 — a 25% reduction is a speed ratio of 3 : 4, so the time ratio is 4 : 3.*

**A6.** (MCQ) Two men cover the same distance, one at 30 km/h and the other at 40 km/h. The slower one takes 2 hours longer. The distance is

- (a) 120 km
- (b) 180 km
- (c) 240 km
- (d) 300 km

*Hint: §1 — d/30 − d/40 = 2. Or: time ratio 4 : 3, difference 1 part = 2 hours.*

## Set B — Average speed and multi-sector journeys

**B1.** (TITA) A car goes from A to B at 60 km/h and returns at 40 km/h. Find the average speed for the whole trip (in km/h).

*Hint: §3 — equal distances, so the harmonic mean 2ab/(a + b). The arithmetic mean 50 is the decoy.*

**B2.** (MCQ) A man drives at 30 km/h for 2 hours and then at 50 km/h for 2 hours. His average speed is

- (a) 37.5 km/h
- (b) 40 km/h
- (c) 41.67 km/h
- (d) 45 km/h

*Hint: §3 — equal times, not equal distances. Here the ordinary average is correct.*

**B3.** (TITA) A man covers three equal distances at 10, 20 and 60 km/h. Find his average speed (in km/h).

*Hint: §3 — three equal distances: 3abc/(ab + bc + ca). Or assume the LCM 60 for each leg.*

**B4.** (MCQ) A person travels 150 km in 5 hours, partly at 20 km/h and partly at 40 km/h. The distance covered at 40 km/h is

- (a) 50 km
- (b) 75 km
- (c) 100 km
- (d) 125 km

*Hint: §3b, algebraic constraint — let x be the distance at 20 km/h, then answer the question actually asked.*

**B5.** (TITA) A man travels the first half of a 60 km journey at 20 km/h and the second half at 30 km/h. Find the total time taken (in hours).

*Hint: §3b — tabulate each leg and sum the times. Do not average the speeds first.*

**B6.** (MCQ) A journey of 90 km is done in three equal legs at 30, 45 and 90 km/h. The average speed is

- (a) 45 km/h
- (b) 50 km/h
- (c) 55 km/h
- (d) 60 km/h

*Hint: §3 — three equal distances again. Assume 90 km total ⟹ each leg 30 km.*

## Set C — Relative speed, chases and meetings

**C1.** (TITA) A thief 300 m ahead runs at 9 km/h; a policeman chases at 12 km/h. After how many seconds is the thief caught?

*Hint: §4 — same direction, so relative speed is the difference. Convert to m/s before dividing.*

**C2.** (MCQ) Two cyclists 120 km apart ride toward each other at 15 km/h and 25 km/h. They meet after

- (a) 2 hours
- (b) 2.5 hours
- (c) 3 hours
- (d) 4 hours

*Hint: §4 — opposite directions, so the speeds add.*

**C3.** (TITA) Two cars start from the same point in the same direction at 50 km/h and 65 km/h. After how many hours are they 45 km apart?

*Hint: §4 — the gap grows at the relative speed.*

**C4.** (MCQ) A car overtakes a bus that is 3 km ahead, in 20 minutes. If the bus travels at 45 km/h, the car's speed is

- (a) 48 km/h
- (b) 50 km/h
- (c) 54 km/h
- (d) 60 km/h

*Hint: §4 — find the relative speed first, then add the bus's speed. 20 minutes is 1/3 hour.*

**C5.** (TITA) A and B start walking toward each other from points 84 km apart at 4 km/h and 3 km/h. A dog runs continuously between them at 10 km/h. Find the total distance the dog covers (in km).

*Hint: §4 — you don't need the dog's path at all, only how long it runs. Find the meeting time first.*

## Set D — Trains

**D1.** (TITA) A train 240 m long crosses a pole in 12 seconds. Find its speed in km/h.

*Hint: §5 — crossing a pole means covering exactly the train's own length. Convert with 18/5.*

**D2.** (MCQ) A train 180 m long crosses a platform 270 m long in 25 seconds. Its speed is

- (a) 54 km/h
- (b) 60 km/h
- (c) 64.8 km/h
- (d) 72 km/h

*Hint: §5 — crossing a platform means train length + platform length.*

**D3.** (TITA) Two trains of lengths 150 m and 250 m travel in opposite directions at 45 km/h and 27 km/h. Find the time (in seconds) to cross each other.

*Hint: §5 — opposite directions, so speeds add; both lengths count.*

**D4.** (MCQ) A 250 m train running at 90 km/h overtakes a cyclist riding at 18 km/h in the same direction. The time taken is

- (a) 10 s
- (b) 12.5 s
- (c) 15 s
- (d) 20 s

*Hint: §5 — the cyclist has no length, so only the train's length is covered, at the relative speed.*

**D5.** (TITA) Two trains running in the same direction at 72 km/h and 54 km/h cross each other in 40 seconds. If the faster train is 120 m long, find the length of the slower train (in metres).

*Hint: §5 — same direction, so the relative speed is the difference; the distance covered is the sum of the lengths.*

**D6.** (MCQ) A train crosses a pole in 15 seconds and a 300 m platform in 30 seconds. The length of the train is

- (a) 200 m
- (b) 250 m
- (c) 300 m
- (d) 450 m

*Hint: §5 — the speed is the same in both cases. Equate L/15 and (L + 300)/30.*

## Set E — Boats and streams

**E1.** (TITA) A boat covers 36 km downstream in 3 hours and the same distance upstream in 6 hours. Find the speed of the stream (in km/h).

*Hint: §6 — get D and U first, then s = (D − U)/2.*

**E2.** (MCQ) A man rows at 8 km/h in still water; the stream flows at 2 km/h. He rows to a place 24 km away and returns. The total time is

- (a) 6 hours
- (b) 6.4 hours
- (c) 6.5 hours
- (d) 7 hours

*Hint: §6 — D = 10, U = 6. Add the two leg times; never average the speeds.*

**E3.** (TITA) A boat's downstream speed is 15 km/h and its upstream speed is 9 km/h. Find its speed in still water (in km/h).

*Hint: §6 — b = (D + U)/2. One of the two formulas worth memorising.*

**E4.** (MCQ) A man takes 3 times as long to row upstream as downstream. The ratio of his speed in still water to the speed of the stream is

- (a) 2 : 1
- (b) 3 : 1
- (c) 4 : 1
- (d) 3 : 2

*Hint: §6 — time ratio m : n gives b : s = (m + n) : (m − n).*

**E5.** (TITA) A boat covers 30 km upstream and 44 km downstream in 10 hours; and 40 km upstream and 55 km downstream in 13 hours. Find the speed of the boat in still water (in km/h).

*Hint: §6 — substitute u = 1/U and d = 1/D to turn this into a linear pair, then solve.*

**E6.** (MCQ) A boat's speed in still water is 10 km/h and the stream flows at 2 km/h. Its average speed for a round trip is

- (a) 9.6 km/h
- (b) 10 km/h
- (c) 10.4 km/h
- (d) 12 km/h

*Hint: §6 — the round-trip average is (b² − s²)/b, and it is always strictly less than b.*

## Set F — Circular tracks

**F1.** (TITA) Two runners on a 400 m circular track run at 8 m/s and 12 m/s in opposite directions from the same point. After how many seconds do they first meet?

*Hint: §9 step 4 — opposite directions, so together they cover one full lap.*

**F2.** (MCQ) Two runners on a circular track run at 8 m/s and 12 m/s in the same direction from the same point. The number of distinct meeting points on the track is

- (a) 1
- (b) 2
- (c) 4
- (d) 5

*Hint: §9 step 1 — reduce the ratio first. Same direction gives a − b.*

**F3.** (TITA) Two runners on a 600 m track run at 5 m/s and 10 m/s in the same direction from the same point. After how many seconds do they meet at the starting point for the first time?

*Hint: §9 — "at the starting point" is the LCM of the lap times, a completely different formula from "anywhere".*

**F4.** (MCQ) On a 900 m circular track, A runs at 15 m/s and B at 25 m/s in opposite directions from the same point. The number of distinct meeting points is

- (a) 2
- (b) 5
- (c) 8
- (d) 40

*Hint: §9 — reduce 15 : 25 first, then add.*

**F5.** (TITA) Three runners start together from the same point on a 300 m circular track at 3, 5 and 7 m/s, all in the same direction. After how many seconds are all three together again?

*Hint: §9, three runners — compute the pairwise meeting times, then take their LCM.*

**F6.** (MCQ) On a 400 m track, A and B start together in the same direction at 6 m/s and 10 m/s. B first gains a full lap on A after

- (a) 40 s
- (b) 66.67 s
- (c) 100 s
- (d) 200 s

*Hint: §9 — gaining a lap means the relative distance covered equals the circumference.*

## Set G — Bouncing meetings and the √ trick

**G1.** (TITA) Two runners start from opposite ends of a 400 m stretch at 6 m/s and 10 m/s and keep bouncing back and forth. After how many seconds do they meet for the 4th time?

*Hint: §7 — the nth meeting needs a combined distance of (2n − 1)D.*

**G2.** (MCQ) A and B start toward each other from P and Q. After meeting, A takes 9 hours to reach Q and B takes 16 hours to reach P. They met after

- (a) 10 hours
- (b) 12 hours
- (c) 12.5 hours
- (d) 25 hours

*Hint: §8 — t = √(t₁ t₂). Note that 25 (the sum) is planted as an option.*

**G3.** (TITA) A and B move toward each other from P and Q. After they meet, A takes 4 hours to reach Q and B takes 25 hours to reach P. Find the ratio of A's speed to B's speed, as a decimal.

*Hint: §8 — Speed A : Speed B = √t₂ : √t₁. The inversion is the whole question.*

**G4.** (MCQ) Two people start from opposite ends of a 240 m track at 4 m/s and 8 m/s and keep bouncing. Their 2nd meeting occurs after

- (a) 20 s
- (b) 40 s
- (c) 60 s
- (d) 80 s

*Hint: §7 — 2nd meeting means a combined distance of 3D, not 2D.*

**G5.** (TITA) A and B start toward each other at the same time from P and Q, 210 km apart. After meeting, A takes 4 hours to reach Q. If A's speed is 30 km/h, find B's speed (in km/h).

*Hint: §8 — the leg A still has to cover after meeting is exactly the leg B had already covered. Find it, then find the pre-meeting time.*

## Set H — Bearings and perpendicular motion

**H1.** (TITA) A cyclist rides north at 9 km/h and another rides east at 12 km/h from the same point at the same time. How far apart are they (in km) after 3 hours?

*Hint: §3c — perpendicular motion, so use Pythagoras on the positions, not relative speed. Look for the 3-4-5 triple.*

**H2.** (MCQ) Two ships leave the same port at the same time, one sailing due south at 24 km/h and the other due west at 7 km/h. After 2 hours they are

- (a) 31 km apart
- (b) 46 km apart
- (c) 50 km apart
- (d) 62 km apart

*Hint: §3c — 7-24-25 is a Pythagorean triple. Option (a) is the sum of the speeds ×1 — the additive error.*

**H3.** (TITA) A is at the origin moving north at 12 km/h. B starts 15 km east of A and moves north at 2 km/h. Find the distance between them (in km) after 2 hours.

*Hint: §3c, different start points — write both positions, take the coordinate differences, then Pythagoras.*

**H4.** (MCQ) Ship P is at (0, 0) sailing east at 10 km/h. Ship Q is at (0, 40) sailing south at 30 km/h. Their separation is least at

- (a) t = 0.8 h
- (b) t = 1.0 h
- (c) t = 1.2 h
- (d) t = 1.5 h

*Hint: §3c, closest approach — write D² (never D) as a quadratic in t and use t = −b/2a.*

**H5.** (TITA) Two men start from the same point along roads inclined at 60° to each other, walking at 5 km/h and 8 km/h. Find the distance between them (in km) after 1 hour.

*Hint: §3c, non-perpendicular bearings — the cosine rule, with cos 60° = ½.*

# Answer key

## Set A

**A1 — 40 minutes.** Time ratio (new : usual) = 6 : 5. The difference of 1 part is 8 minutes ⟹ usual time = 5 parts = **40 minutes**.

**A2 — (c) 72 min.** Time ratio (new : usual) = 5 : 6 ⟹ difference of 1 part = 12 min ⟹ usual = 6 parts = **72 min**.

**A3 — 5 km.** d/4 − d/5 = 15/60 ⟹ d/20 = 1/4 ⟹ d = **5 km**. (The gap is 5 + 10 = 15 minutes, not 5.)

**A4 — (b) 60 min.** Time ratio (new : usual) = 5 : 3 ⟹ difference of 2 parts = 40 min ⟹ 1 part = 20 ⟹ usual = 3 parts = **60 min**.

**A5 — 60 minutes.** Time ratio (new : usual) = 4 : 3 ⟹ difference of 1 part = 20 min ⟹ usual = 3 parts = **60 minutes**.

**A6 — (c) 240 km.** d/30 − d/40 = 2 ⟹ d/120 = 2 ⟹ d = **240 km**. Check: 8 hours and 6 hours ✓

## Set B

**B1 — 48 km/h.** 2(60)(40)/(60 + 40) = 4800/100 = **48 km/h**.

**B2 — (b) 40 km/h.** Equal times ⟹ average = (30 + 50)/2 = **40 km/h**. (37.5 is the harmonic mean — the equal-distance answer, planted as a decoy.)

**B3 — 18 km/h.** 3(10)(20)(60)/(200 + 1200 + 600) = 36000/2000 = **18 km/h**. Check with LCM: 60 km legs take 6 + 3 + 1 = 10 hours for 180 km ✓

**B4 — (c) 100 km.** x/20 + (150 − x)/40 = 5 ⟹ 2x + 150 − x = 200 ⟹ x = 50 km at 20 km/h ⟹ at 40 km/h he covers **100 km**. (50 km is the other leg — the trap.)

**B5 — 2.5 hours.** 30/20 + 30/30 = 1.5 + 1 = **2.5 hours**. (Average speed = 60/2.5 = 24 km/h, which is 2ab/(a+b) ✓)

**B6 — (a) 45 km/h.** Times: 1 + 2/3 + 1/3 = 2 hours for 90 km ⟹ **45 km/h**. (The arithmetic mean of 30, 45, 90 is 55 — the decoy.)

## Set C

**C1 — 360 seconds.** Relative speed = 3 km/h = 3 × 5/18 = 5/6 m/s ⟹ time = 300 ÷ 5/6 = **360 seconds** (6 minutes).

**C2 — (c) 3 hours.** Relative speed = 40 km/h ⟹ time = 120/40 = **3 hours**.

**C3 — 3 hours.** Gap grows at 15 km/h ⟹ 45/15 = **3 hours**.

**C4 — (c) 54 km/h.** Relative speed = 3 ÷ (1/3) = 9 km/h ⟹ car = 45 + 9 = **54 km/h**.

**C5 — 120 km.** They meet after 84/(4 + 3) = 12 hours ⟹ the dog runs for 12 hours at 10 km/h = **120 km**.

## Set D

**D1 — 72 km/h.** Speed = 240/12 = 20 m/s = 20 × 18/5 = **72 km/h**.

**D2 — (c) 64.8 km/h.** Distance = 450 m in 25 s = 18 m/s = **64.8 km/h**. (Using only the 180 m gives 25.92 km/h — the classic slip.)

**D3 — 20 seconds.** Relative speed = 72 km/h = 20 m/s; distance = 400 m ⟹ time = **20 seconds**.

**D4 — (b) 12.5 s.** Relative speed = 72 km/h = 20 m/s ⟹ time = 250/20 = **12.5 seconds**.

**D5 — 80 m.** Relative speed = 18 km/h = 5 m/s ⟹ combined length = 5 × 40 = 200 m ⟹ slower train = 200 − 120 = **80 m**.

**D6 — (c) 300 m.** L/15 = (L + 300)/30 ⟹ 2L = L + 300 ⟹ L = **300 m**. (Speed = 20 m/s in both ✓)

## Set E

**E1 — 3 km/h.** D = 12 km/h, U = 6 km/h ⟹ s = (12 − 6)/2 = **3 km/h** (and b = 9 km/h).

**E2 — (b) 6.4 hours.** 24/10 + 24/6 = 2.4 + 4 = **6.4 hours**. (Round-trip average = 48/6.4 = 7.5 km/h = (b² − s²)/b ✓)

**E3 — 12 km/h.** b = (15 + 9)/2 = **12 km/h**.

**E4 — (a) 2 : 1.** m : n = 3 : 1 ⟹ b : s = 4 : 2 = **2 : 1**. (Answering 3 : 1 is using the time ratio directly — the trap.)

**E5 — 8 km/h.** 30u + 44d = 10 and 40u + 55d = 13. Multiply the first by 4/3 and subtract: 11/(3D) = 1/3 ⟹ D = 11. Then 30/U = 6 ⟹ U = 5. b = (11 + 5)/2 = **8 km/h** (s = 3).

**E6 — (a) 9.6 km/h.** (100 − 4)/10 = **9.6 km/h**. It must be below 10 because more time is spent going upstream.

## Set F

**F1 — 20 seconds.** 400/(8 + 12) = **20 seconds**.

**F2 — (a) 1.** 8 : 12 = 2 : 3 (coprime) ⟹ meeting points = 3 − 2 = **1**, so they only ever meet at the start. (Not reducing gives 12 − 8 = 4 — the trap.)

**F3 — 120 seconds.** Lap times = 120 s and 60 s ⟹ LCM = **120 seconds**. (Meeting anywhere would be 600/5 = 120 s too, because the reduced ratio 1 : 2 gives just one meeting point.)

**F4 — (c) 8.** 15 : 25 = 3 : 5 ⟹ 3 + 5 = **8 meeting points**, spaced 112.5 m apart. (Forgetting to reduce gives 40 — an option, deliberately.)

**F5 — 150 seconds.** Pairwise gaps 2, 2 and 4 m/s ⟹ meeting times 150 s, 150 s, 75 s ⟹ LCM = **150 seconds**. Check: distances 450, 750, 1050 all reduce to the 150 m mark ✓

**F6 — (c) 100 s.** 400/(10 − 6) = **100 seconds**.

## Set G

**G1 — 175 seconds.** Combined distance = (2×4 − 1)(400) = 2800 m at 16 m/s ⟹ **175 seconds**.

**G2 — (b) 12 hours.** t = √(9 × 16) = **12 hours**.

**G3 — 2.5.** A : B = √25 : √4 = 5 : 2 = **2.5**. A has the shorter remaining time, so A must be the faster runner — a free sanity check.

**G4 — (c) 60 s.** 3 × 240 = 720 m at 12 m/s ⟹ **60 seconds**. (Using 2D gives 40 s — an option, deliberately.)

**G5 — 40 km/h.** After the meeting A covers 4 × 30 = 120 km, so B had covered 120 km before the meeting and A had covered 90 km. Pre-meeting time = 90/30 = 3 hours ⟹ B's speed = 120/3 = **40 km/h**. Check: 30 + 40 = 70 and 210/70 = 3 ✓

## Set H

**H1 — 45 km.** t√(u² + v²) = 3 × √(81 + 144) = 3 × 15 = **45 km**.

**H2 — (c) 50 km apart.** 2 × √(49 + 576) = 2 × 25 = **50 km**.

**H3 — 25 km.** After 2 h: A = (0, 24), B = (15, 4). Separation = √(15² + 20²) = **25 km**.

**H4 — (c) t = 1.2 h.** D² = 100t² + (40 − 30t)² = 1000t² − 2400t + 1600 ⟹ minimum at t = 2400/2000 = **1.2 hours** (D = 4√10 ≈ 12.65 km).

**H5 — 7 km.** D² = 25 + 64 − 2(5)(8)(½) = 89 − 40 = 49 ⟹ D = **7 km**. (Using Pythagoras here would give √89 ≈ 9.43 — wrong, because the angle isn't 90°.)

## Mixed Set

**M1 — (c) 72 min.** Time ratio 7 : 6 ⟹ 1 part = 12 min ⟹ usual = 6 parts = **72 min**. (84 = 7 parts is the new time — the trap.)

**M2 — 72 km/h.** 800/40 = 20 m/s = **72 km/h**.

**M3 — (c) 3.5 km/h.** D = 15/1.25 = 12 km/h, U = 15/3 = 5 km/h ⟹ s = (12 − 5)/2 = **3.5 km/h**.

**M4 — 6 seconds.** Relative speed = 144 km/h = 40 m/s; distance = 240 m ⟹ **6 seconds**.

**M5 — (b) 48 km/h.** 2(40)(60)/100 = **48 km/h**. (50 is the arithmetic mean — the standing decoy.)

**M6 — 48 km.** Time = 120/50 = 2.4 h ⟹ A covers 20 × 2.4 = **48 km**. (72 km is the distance from B's town.)

**M7 — (b) 5.** 20 : 30 = 2 : 3 ⟹ **5 meeting points**. (Unreduced gives 50 — an option.)

**M8 — 4 km.** d/5 − d/6 = 8/60 ⟹ d/30 = 2/15 ⟹ d = **4 km**.

**M9 — (b) 6 : 5.** A : B = √36 : √25 = **6 : 5**. A finishes sooner, so A is faster — the check that catches the inversion.

**M10 — 450 seconds.** Combined distance = 9 × 500 = 4500 m at 10 m/s ⟹ **450 seconds**.

---

**Common error audit** — if you got a question wrong, find it here before moving on:

| Question | The error it is designed to catch |
|---|---|
| A1, A2, A4, A5, M1 | using the speed ratio as the time ratio, or reading the wrong part as the answer (§1) |
| A3, M8 | treating a "late by x, early by y" pair as a gap of x instead of x + y |
| B1, B6, M5 | using the arithmetic mean where equal distances demand the harmonic mean (§3) |
| B2 | using the harmonic mean where the times, not the distances, are equal |
| B4 | solving for the wrong leg and reporting it |
| C1, D1, D3, M2, M4 | mixing km/h with metres instead of converting by 5/18 first (§2) |
| C5 | trying to sum the dog's zig-zag legs instead of using the total running time |
| D2, D6 | forgetting the platform length, or the train's own length |
| D4, D5 | using the wrong relative speed for the stated direction (§5) |
| E2, E6 | averaging the two leg speeds for a round trip instead of (b² − s²)/b (§6) |
| E4 | using the time ratio directly as b : s instead of (m + n) : (m − n) |
| F2, F4, M7 | not reducing the speed ratio to lowest terms before counting meeting points (§9) |
| F3 | using the "meet anywhere" formula where "meet at the start" needs the LCM of lap times |
| G1, G4, M10 | using nD instead of (2n − 1)D for the nth bounce meeting (§7) |
| G2, G3, M9 | inverting the √ trick — Speed A : Speed B = √t₂ : √t₁, not √t₁ : √t₂ (§8) |
| H1, H2, H3 | adding distances instead of applying Pythagoras to perpendicular motion (§3c) |
| H4 | minimising D instead of D², which turns a clean quadratic into a mess |
| H5 | using Pythagoras where the angle between the paths is not 90° — use the cosine rule |
