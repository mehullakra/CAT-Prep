# Percentages — Practice Sets

> Companion to `Percentages.md`. Hints point to the section of the notes that contains the intended method — read the hint only after you've been stuck for a minute. Answer key with short solutions is at the bottom.
>
> **TITA** = type-in-the-answer (no options). **MCQ** = choose one.
>
> Suggested timing: 1 min/question for Sets A–F, and attempt the Mixed Set in one timed block.
>
> **Name the base before you compute.** Almost every wrong answer in this topic is a right calculation on the wrong base (§3).
>
> Work in multiplying factors, not in additions: +25% is ×5/4, −20% is ×4/5. Chains then collapse in one line (§5).

---

## Set A — Fraction ↔ percentage fluency

**A1.** (TITA) Find 37.5% of 1,024.

*Hint: §2 — 37.5% is 3/8. Never long-multiply by 0.375.*

**A2.** (MCQ) If 15% of a number is 45, then 40% of the same number is

- (a) 90
- (b) 105
- (c) 120
- (d) 135

*Hint: §1 — find the number first, or scale directly: 40/15 = 8/3.*

**A3.** (TITA) What percentage of 250 is 45?

*Hint: §3 — "of 250" names the base. It goes in the denominator.*

**A4.** (MCQ) 62.5% of 800 equals

- (a) 480
- (b) 500
- (c) 520
- (d) 560

*Hint: §2 — 62.5% is 5/8.*

**A5.** (TITA) A number increased by 25% gives 350. Find the original number.

*Hint: §3 — divide by 1.25, don't subtract 25% of 350. The base is the original, not the result.*

**A6.** (MCQ) If A is 300 and B is 240, then B is less than A by

- (a) 20%
- (b) 25%
- (c) 30%
- (d) 33.33%

*Hint: §3 — "less than A" makes A the base, so divide by 300, not 240.*

## Set B — The reciprocal relationship

**B1.** (MCQ) If A is 25% more than B, then B is less than A by

- (a) 20%
- (b) 25%
- (c) 16.67%
- (d) 33.33%

*Hint: §4 — 25% is 1/4 more, so the reverse is 1/5 less.*

**B2.** (TITA) If A's income is 33.33% more than B's, by what percent is B's income less than A's?

*Hint: §4 table — 1/3 more corresponds to 1/4 less.*

**B3.** (MCQ) If the price of sugar is 20% less than that of rice, the price of rice is more than that of sugar by

- (a) 20%
- (b) 25%
- (c) 16.67%
- (d) 30%

*Hint: §4 — 1/5 less reverses to 1/4 more.*

**B4.** (TITA) A's salary is 20% less than B's. By what percent is B's salary more than A's?

*Hint: §4 — same pair as B3, stated the other way round.*

**B5.** (MCQ) If X is 60% of Y, then Y exceeds X by

- (a) 40%
- (b) 60%
- (c) 66.67%
- (d) 150%

*Hint: §4 — the base flips from Y to X. Y/X = 5/3.*

## Set C — Successive changes

**C1.** (TITA) The price of an article rises 20% and then falls 20%. Find the net percentage change (enter a negative number for a fall).

*Hint: §5 — chain the factors. Equal up-then-down percentages never cancel.*

**C2.** (MCQ) A salary is increased by 25% and then decreased by 20%. The net change is

- (a) 5% increase
- (b) no change
- (c) 5% decrease
- (d) 4% decrease

*Hint: §5 — 5/4 × 4/5. The fractions are reciprocals, which is exactly why this pair does cancel.*

**C3.** (TITA) A town's population rises 10% in the first year, 20% in the second and 25% in the third, reaching 33,000. Find the population 3 years ago.

*Hint: §6 — chain the three factors, then divide. Do not subtract 55%.*

**C4.** (MCQ) The length of a rectangle increases by 20% and the breadth decreases by 10%. The area changes by

- (a) +8%
- (b) +10%
- (c) −8%
- (d) +2%

*Hint: §5 — area is a product, so multiply the factors. The naive +20 − 10 = +10 is planted.*

**C5.** (TITA) A quantity is increased by 30% and then increased by 30% again. Find the total percentage increase.

*Hint: §5 — 1.3², not 60%.*

**C6.** (MCQ) After two successive discounts of 10% and 20%, an article sells for ₹1,440. Its marked price was

- (a) ₹1,800
- (b) ₹2,000
- (c) ₹2,100
- (d) ₹2,400

*Hint: §5 — the combined factor is 0.72, not 0.70. Then divide.*

## Set D — Price and consumption (product held constant)

**D1.** (TITA) The price of petrol rises 25%. By what percent must consumption be reduced to keep expenditure unchanged?

*Hint: §7 — expenditure = price × consumption, so the two factors must be reciprocal. 25% up is 1/4; the cut is 1/5.*

**D2.** (MCQ) The price of rice falls 20%. By what percent can a family increase consumption without increasing expenditure?

- (a) 20%
- (b) 25%
- (c) 16.67%
- (d) 33.33%

*Hint: §7 in the other direction — 1/0.8.*

**D3.** (TITA) The price of sugar rises from ₹30/kg to ₹36/kg. By what percent must a family reduce consumption so that expenditure rises by only 10%? Give the answer to two decimals.

*Hint: §7, generalised — the consumption factor is (target expenditure factor)/(price factor).*

**D4.** (MCQ) A man's salary rises 20% and his expenditure rises 10%. If his savings were 20% of his salary, his savings rise by

- (a) 40%
- (b) 50%
- (c) 60%
- (d) 70%

*Hint: §1 — assume a salary of 100, tabulate before and after. Savings are a residue, so they move much faster than either component.*

**D5.** (TITA) If the price of an article is reduced by 20%, a man can buy 5 kg more for ₹600. Find the original price per kg (in ₹).

*Hint: §7 — write the quantity at each price as 600/p and 600/0.8p, then take the difference.*

## Set E — Percentage points, remainders and percentages of percentages

**E1.** (MCQ) A bank raises its interest rate from 4% to 5%. This is

- (a) a 1% increase
- (b) a 25% increase
- (c) a 20% increase
- (d) a 5% increase

*Hint: §8 — 1 percentage point is not 1%. The base for a percentage change is the old rate.*

**E2.** (TITA) A man spends 30% of his income on rent and 20% of the remainder on food. What percentage of his income is left?

*Hint: §9 — "of the remainder" resets the base to what is left, not to the original.*

**E3.** (MCQ) In a school 60% of the students are boys, and 25% of the boys wear glasses. The percentage of the whole school that is boys wearing glasses is

- (a) 12%
- (b) 15%
- (c) 20%
- (d) 25%

*Hint: §12 — a percentage of a percentage is a product.*

**E4.** (TITA) A man spends 25% of his salary on rent, 30% of the remainder on food, and saves the rest, which is ₹5,250. Find his salary (in ₹).

*Hint: §9 — chain 0.75 and 0.70 to get the saved fraction, then divide.*

**E5.** (MCQ) The pass percentage in an exam rose from 40% to 48%. Expressed as a percentage of the earlier figure, the increase is

- (a) 8%
- (b) 16.67%
- (c) 20%
- (d) 25%

*Hint: §8 — the rise is 8 percentage points; the question asks for the relative change.*

## Set F — Elections, exams and pass marks

**F1.** (TITA) In an election between two candidates, the winner got 60% of the votes and won by 1,200 votes. Find the total number of votes polled.

*Hint: §10 — the margin is 60 − 40 = 20 percentage points of the total.*

**F2.** (MCQ) A student scores 30% and fails by 30 marks. Another scores 45% and gets 15 marks more than the pass mark. The maximum marks are

- (a) 250
- (b) 300
- (c) 350
- (d) 400

*Hint: §10 — the gap between the two scores is 45 marks and 15 percentage points. Subtract the two equations.*

**F3.** (TITA) A candidate needs 40% to pass. He scores 160 marks and fails by 40 marks. Find the maximum marks.

*Hint: §10 — the pass mark is what he scored plus what he was short by.*

**F4.** (MCQ) In an election, 20% of the registered voters did not vote. The winner got 60% of the votes cast and won by 3,200 votes. The number of registered voters is

- (a) 16,000
- (b) 20,000
- (c) 24,000
- (d) 25,000

*Hint: §10 — two bases here: the margin is a percentage of the votes cast, which is itself a percentage of the registered voters.*

**F5.** (TITA) In an exam, 35% failed in Maths, 45% failed in English and 20% failed in both. What percentage passed in both subjects?

*Hint: §10 — inclusion–exclusion for those who failed at least one, then take the complement.*

**F6.** (MCQ) In an election among three candidates, A got 45% and B got 35% of the votes. If C got 4,000 votes, the total number of votes was

- (a) 16,000
- (b) 18,000
- (c) 20,000
- (d) 24,000

*Hint: §10 — C's share is the residue.*

## Mixed Set — exam feel (13 minutes)

**M1.** (MCQ) If the price of a commodity increases by 60%, by what percent must consumption be reduced to keep expenditure unchanged?

- (a) 33.33%
- (b) 37.5%
- (c) 40%
- (d) 60%

**M2.** (TITA) Find 40% of 60% of 250.

**M3.** (MCQ) A number is increased by 20% and the result is then decreased by 25%. The net change is

- (a) 5% decrease
- (b) 10% decrease
- (c) 5% increase
- (d) no change

**M4.** (TITA) A man's income rises 25% while his tax rate falls from 20% to 16%. Find the percentage change in the tax he pays (enter 0 if unchanged).

**M5.** (MCQ) If A's salary is 20% more than B's, and B's is 25% more than C's, then A's salary exceeds C's by

- (a) 45%
- (b) 50%
- (c) 55%
- (d) 45.5%

**M6.** (TITA) A salary is first increased by 20% and then decreased by 20%. If the final salary is ₹19,200, find the original salary (in ₹).

**M7.** (MCQ) In a class of 50, 40% are girls. If 10 more girls join, the percentage of girls becomes

- (a) 45%
- (b) 48%
- (c) 50%
- (d) 55%

**M8.** (TITA) A shopkeeper reduces the price of an item by 20%, which increases sales volume by 30%. Find the percentage change in revenue.

**M9.** (MCQ) A man spends 75% of his income. If his income rises 20% and his expenditure rises 10%, his savings rise by

- (a) 10%
- (b) 25%
- (c) 50%
- (d) 60%

**M10.** (TITA) In an exam a candidate scoring 25% fails by 30 marks, while one scoring 50% gets 20 marks more than the pass mark. Find the pass mark.

# Answer key

## Set A

**A1 — 384.** 3/8 × 1024 = **384**.

**A2 — (c) 120.** The number is 45/0.15 = 300 ⟹ 40% = **120**. Or 45 × (40/15) = 120.

**A3 — 18%.** 45/250 × 100 = **18%**.

**A4 — (b) 500.** 5/8 × 800 = **500**.

**A5 — 280.** 350/1.25 = **280**. (Subtracting 25% of 350 gives 262.5 — the standard error.)

**A6 — (a) 20%.** 60/300 = **20%**. (Dividing by 240 gives 25% — that would answer "A is more than B by …".)

## Set B

**B1 — (a) 20%.** A : B = 5 : 4 ⟹ B is short by 1 part out of 5 = **20%**.

**B2 — 25%.** A : B = 4 : 3 ⟹ B is short by 1 part out of 4 = **25%**.

**B3 — (b) 25%.** Sugar : rice = 4 : 5 ⟹ rice exceeds sugar by 1/4 = **25%**.

**B4 — 25%.** A : B = 4 : 5 ⟹ B exceeds A by 1/4 = **25%**.

**B5 — (c) 66.67%.** X : Y = 3 : 5 ⟹ Y exceeds X by 2/3 = **66.67%**. (40% answers "X is less than Y by …".)

## Set C

**C1 — −4%.** 1.2 × 0.8 = 0.96 ⟹ a net change of **−4%**.

**C2 — (b) no change.** 1.25 × 0.8 = 1 ⟹ **no change**. Contrast C1: it is the fractions, not the percentages, that must be reciprocal.

**C3 — 20000.** 1.1 × 1.2 × 1.25 = 1.65 ⟹ P = 33000/1.65 = **20,000**.

**C4 — (a) +8%.** 1.2 × 0.9 = 1.08 ⟹ **+8%**.

**C5 — 69%.** 1.3² = 1.69 ⟹ **69%**.

**C6 — (b) ₹2,000.** 0.9 × 0.8 = 0.72 ⟹ MP = 1440/0.72 = **₹2,000**. (Using a single 30% discount gives ₹2,057 — not an option, which is your check.)

## Set D

**D1 — 20%.** New consumption factor = 1/1.25 = 0.8 ⟹ reduction = **20%**. (Answering 25% is the standard error.)

**D2 — (b) 25%.** 1/0.8 = 1.25 ⟹ an increase of **25%**.

**D3 — 8.33%.** Price factor 1.2, expenditure factor 1.1 ⟹ consumption factor = 1.1/1.2 = 11/12 ⟹ reduction = 1/12 = **8.33%**.

**D4 — (c) 60%.** Before: 100, 80, savings 20. After: 120, 88, savings 32 ⟹ increase = 12/20 = **60%**.

**D5 — ₹30.** 600/0.8p − 600/p = 5 ⟹ 750/p − 600/p = 5 ⟹ 150/p = 5 ⟹ p = **₹30**. Check: 20 kg vs 25 kg ✓

## Set E

**E1 — (b) a 25% increase.** 1/4 = **a 25% increase** (or equivalently, a rise of 1 percentage point).

**E2 — 56%.** 0.7 × 0.8 = 0.56 ⟹ **56%**. (Treating both as percentages of income gives 50% — the planted error.)

**E3 — (b) 15%.** 0.6 × 0.25 = 0.15 ⟹ **15%**.

**E4 — 10000.** 0.75 × 0.7 = 0.525 ⟹ salary = 5250/0.525 = **₹10,000**.

**E5 — (c) 20%.** 8/40 = **20%**. (8% would be the percentage-point answer.)

## Set F

**F1 — 6000.** Margin = 20% of total = 1200 ⟹ total = **6,000**.

**F2 — (b) 300.** 0.45M − 0.30M = 30 + 15 = 45 ⟹ 0.15M = 45 ⟹ M = **300**. (Pass mark = 90 + 30 = 120 ✓)

**F3 — 500.** Pass mark = 160 + 40 = 200 = 0.4M ⟹ M = **500**.

**F4 — (b) 20,000.** Margin = 20% of votes cast = 0.20 × 0.80V = 0.16V = 3200 ⟹ V = **20,000**. (Ignoring the turnout gives 16,000 — an option.)

**F5 — 40%.** Failed at least one = 35 + 45 − 20 = 60% ⟹ passed both = **40%**.

**F6 — (c) 20,000.** C = 100 − 45 − 35 = 20% ⟹ total = 4000/0.20 = **20,000**.

## Mixed Set

**M1 — (b) 37.5%.** 1/1.6 = 0.625 ⟹ a reduction of **37.5%**.

**M2 — 60.** 0.24 × 250 = **60**.

**M3 — (b) 10% decrease.** 1.2 × 0.75 = 0.9 ⟹ a **10% decrease**.

**M4 — 0% (unchanged).** 1.25 × (16/20) = 1.25 × 0.8 = 1 ⟹ the tax is **unchanged**. (100 × 0.20 = 20 and 125 × 0.16 = 20 ✓)

**M5 — (b) 50%.** 1.2 × 1.25 = 1.5 ⟹ **50%**.

**M6 — 20000.** 19200/0.96 = **₹20,000**.

**M7 — (c) 50%.** Girls 20, boys 30. After: 30 girls out of 60 = **50%**. (Forgetting to update the total gives 60%.)

**M8 — +4%.** 0.8 × 1.3 = 1.04 ⟹ revenue rises by **4%**.

**M9 — (c) 50%.** Before: 100, 75, savings 25. After: 120, 82.5, savings 37.5 ⟹ increase = 12.5/25 = **50%**.

**M10 — 80.** 0.50M − 0.25M = 30 + 20 = 50 ⟹ M = 200. Pass mark = 0.25(200) + 30 = **80**. Check: 100 − 20 = 80 ✓

**Common error audit** — if you got a question wrong, find it here before moving on:

| Question | The error it is designed to catch |
|---|---|
| A3, A5, A6, E5 | computing on the wrong base — the phrase "of" or "than" always names it (§3) |
| B1–B5 | reversing a percentage comparison by reusing the same number instead of the reciprocal fraction (§4) |
| C1, C4, C5 | adding successive percentage changes instead of multiplying the factors (§5) |
| C2 | assuming equal percentages never cancel — they do when the fractions are reciprocal |
| C3, C6, M6 | subtracting the total percentage from the final value instead of dividing by the chained factor |
| D1, D2, D3, M1 | quoting the price change as the consumption change — they are reciprocal, never equal (§7) |
| D4, M9 | computing on income or expenditure instead of on savings, which is a residue and moves fastest |
| E1, E5 | confusing percentage points with a percentage change (§8) |
| E2, E4 | applying the second percentage to the original rather than to the remainder (§9) |
| F1, F4 | forgetting that the winning margin is the difference of the shares, and that turnout is a second base (§10) |
| F2, M10 | solving one equation at a time instead of subtracting to eliminate the pass mark |
| M7 | changing the numerator but not the denominator |
